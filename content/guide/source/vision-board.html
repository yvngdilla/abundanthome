Hello, my darling co‑creator!

By the end of this workbook you will have three things: one page that describes the life you are manifesting, a vision board built from that page, and a spot in your home where it works on you every day.

Most vision boards start with pictures. Mine start with words. When you know exactly what you want, choosing images is easy, and the board says something specific.

That is the difference between a board that ends up in a drawer and one that changes how you see your days.

Part 1 · 30 minutes

Write it

Set the scene

Give yourself 15 quiet minutes

Phone away, nobody needing you. This is time for you.

  • Wear something you feel good in. Act as if.
  • Sit somewhere that supports your back, facing the door. Not with your back to it. You will feel safer, and you will think bigger.
  • Light a candle or put on music you love, if that helps.
  • Take eight slow breaths, counting to eight on each one.
Now imagine

You already have all the time, money and support you want. Not someday. Today. What does your life look like?

Six questions

Answer quickly. First thoughts are the honest ones.

  1. I want more ______ in my life.
  2. When I have more ______, I will ______, and I will feel ______.
  3. Where do I wake up? The room, the light, what is outside the window.
  4. Who is with me?
  5. How do I spend my days? Work, money, time, rest.
  6. What does my new life look, sound, smell and feel like?

Write your answers on My six answers, page 7.

Write your vision page

Turn your answers into one page

Start with: “I see myself living a life where I...”

  • Write in the present tense, as if it is already true.
  • Walk through one day, from waking up to falling asleep.
  • Say how each part feels. The feeling is the point, not the house or the car.
  • Don’t edit. You can change it anytime.
If a doubtful voice pipes up

Answer it with: “That’s how I used to think.” Then keep writing.

Jenny’s page

Jenny wanted more love. Part of her page read:

“I see myself living a life where I feel loved, easily. I like to give love and receive it. When I feel loved, I take care of myself. I dress in linen, I choose good food, and I sleep in as long as I like.”

Jenny had always believed sleeping in was lazy. Writing it down gave her permission, and things began to shift.

Read your page tonight before bed and again tomorrow morning. Then you are ready to build.

Write it on My vision page, page 8.

Part 2 · About an hour

Build it

Pick your format

The best format is the one you will actually look at

A board

Cork, foam or poster board. The classic. Glue on fabric, paint chips, even a feather.

A notebook

A vision board you can keep adding to. Keep it on your nightstand.

Your phone

Your lock screen, seen fifty times a day. The free template at abundanthomedesign.com makes one in minutes.

I love cutting and gluing because it uses all my senses: the sound of magazine pages, the feel of fabric, a candle burning while I work. But if a phone board is what you will look at, make that.

Five steps to your board
  1. Underline. Read your vision page and underline every place, thing and feeling.
  2. Gather. Find an image, color, texture or word for each underlined line: magazines, printed photos, fabric scraps, a paint chip, a pressed flower. Add something you can smell if you like, a dab of perfume or oil.
  3. Match. Every piece on your board points back to a line on your page. If it doesn’t, leave it off, however pretty it is.
  4. Arrange. Put the image that matters most in the center or at eye level. Group the rest by area of life. Leave some empty space so the board can breathe.
  5. Add three phrases. Pick three short lines from your page, in the present tense. “I wake up to the ocean.” “Money comes easily to me.”

Use Image match (page 9) and My three phrases (page 10).

A designer’s eye

You will look at it every day, so make it beautiful

One focal point

One image should clearly be the star. Everything else supports it.

Three colors

Two or three colors will keep showing up in what you gather. Let them lead, and use them for the background or border.

Mix textures

Smooth photos next to fabric, paper, wood. It makes you want to touch it.

It can be 3D

Nothing has to lie flat or fit a frame. Let things stick out.

Why specific matters: Rose’s cottage

My client Rose had dreamed of a forest cottage for years. Her board had a cottage tucked in green foliage, a little yellow wooden gate, and a sprig of mugwort, her favorite plant.

When her landlord passed away and she had two weeks to move, a friend told her about a rental at the foot of the Santa Cruz mountains. She drove up to a cottage in the trees, with mugwort growing at the edge of the yard and a little wooden gate.

The gate was red. She got the cottage and painted the gate yellow herself.

Part 3 · 10 minutes, then daily

Live with it

Give it a home

Where your board lives matters as much as what is on it

Put it where you will see it in the first ten minutes of your day.

The wall facing your bed
Beside your bathroom mirror
Your phone’s lock screen

Then clear the area around it. Your eyes take in the whole wall, not just the board, so piles and cords next to it tell the old story.

Two minutes a day

Each morning, look at your board for two minutes. Pick one image. Imagine it is already true, and notice how that feels. That’s it.

Let it grow

When something on your board arrives, write it in your Arrivals log (page 11) and celebrate it. Once a month, take off anything that no longer feels true and add what you want next.
