                  MORE THAN A FEELING:

  What Texture Actually IS and Why It’s Important

  Every element we choose for a space brings its own unique qualities to the
  table, contributing to the overall aesthetic and feeling. However, texture is an
  underused element that stands out for its remarkable ability to transform a space
  and make it truly exceptional.

  Texture engages both our visual and tactile senses. This incredibly important
  design element serves a multilayered purpose, breathing life and personality
  into a space.

  For instance, think about how radically different a room with rough, textured walls
  and a weathered leather sofa feels versus one with smooth, polished surfaces
  and crisp linen curtains. Each room looks and feels completely different, creating
  two distinct energies in each space.

  Wood, stone, brick, concrete, plaster, and metal are among the materials capable
  of adding texture to a room. Fabrics offer another avenue for texture, ranging
  from soft and plush textiles to coarser, more textured options.

        There are two distinct types of texture
                within interior design:

  1. Tactile texture
  is about how a surface feels when touched. It encompasses a wide range of
  sensations, from the smoothness of glass or the softness of velvet to the hardness
  of metal or the roughness of natural stone. When you run your hand over a surface,
  the tactile texture is what you perceive.

  2. Visual texture is two-fold:
  Visual texture can be an illusion, appearing different from its actual tactile feel.
  It is two-dimensional (lacking genuine depth) and can be created through color
  or patterns. These are surfaces that imitate the look of other materials but do not
  have the corresponding tactile qualities. Some examples are wood, brick, marble,
  silk, or stone.

  Visual texture can also refer to an actual three-dimensional object or surface.
  Some examples of this are rough stone, baskets, textured mirrors, slab countertops,
  or the texture of a thick frame around a piece of art.

   LET’S TALK ABOUT DEPTH AND APPEAL

  So why does texture make a difference in your space?

  In interior design, texture serves multiple functions. It contributes to balance
  within a space, ensuring that key elements stand out while maintaining an overall
  sense of harmony. Additionally, texture adds visual weight to various elements,
  drawing attention and enhancing the depth and appeal of the design.

  It’s this interplay that is fundamental to creating a visually engaging and well-
  balanced interior environment.

  Here’s a deeper look at why you shouldn’t neglect texture in your home:

  Balance and Contrast:
  Texture helps balance a room’s design. When used effectively, it can create
  harmony between contrasting elements such as hard and soft, rough and smooth,
  shiny and matte. This balance contributes to a visually pleasing composition.

  Function and Comfort:
  Textures are closely linked to the functionality and comfort of a space. For instance,
  soft textures like plush carpets and upholstered furniture can make a room more
  comfortable, while hard textures like wood or metal may serve practical functions
  but need to be balanced with softer elements for comfort.

  Function and Comfort:
  Textures are closely linked to the functionality and comfort of a space. For instance,
  soft textures like plush carpets and upholstered furniture can make a room more
  comfortable, while hard textures like wood or metal may serve practical functions
  but need to be balanced with softer elements for comfort.

  Balance and Contrast:
  Textures can define different areas within a space. For example, using a different
  texture on an accent wall can distinguish it from the surrounding walls, creating
  a focal point.

  Acoustic Considerations:
  Textures can influence the acoustics of a room. Hard, reflective surfaces can
  lead to more echo and noise, while soft, absorptive textures can help with sound
  control.

  Wayfinding and Navigation:
  Textures can be used for wayfinding and navigation within a space. A change in
  texture on the floor, for example, can signal transitions from one area to another.

   KIM K, MUCH? WHY TEXTURE IS EVEN MORE
   IMPORTANT FOR MONOCHROMATIC DESIGN

  I love colorful and eclectic interiors, but if the white, beige, and minimal aesthetic
  is more your cup of tea, introducing texture into your space is going to be a
  game-changer.

  When color choices are limited, texture steps in as the hero, doing a lot of heavy
  lifting to prevent a space from feeling flat and dull. Adding in texture will help to
  introduce layers and depth, making a space visually captivating and interesting.

  Texture also aids in enhancing contrast by allowing deliberate juxtapositions of
  smooth and rough surfaces or light and dark elements. Furthermore, it provides a
  tactile experience, ensuring that even in minimalist spaces, comfort and coziness
  are not compromised.

  Texture brings balance to the potential sterility of minimalism, infusing warmth
  and personality. It can also be used as a tool for highlighting key elements and
  creating an overall sense of tranquility, making the space inviting and serene
  while adhering to minimalist principles of simplicity and functionality.

  Take my friend’s living room as an example. It was undeniably beautiful and sleek,
  yet something was a little “off” - and she couldn’t quite put her finger on what it
  was.

  In an instant, I pinpointed the issue –- her space was lacking texture.

  So, we introduced a plush area rug and textured throw pillows, instantly softening
  the space and adding some visual intrigue, while maintaining the chic minimalistic
  vibe she loved.

  With just a few simple changes, her space went from cold and uncomfortable to
  warm and welcoming. OH BABY!!!

    Identifying Textural Components
            in Interior Design

   So, you’ve learned a lot about what texture is and why it’s important. But where can you
   use it to amplify the design of your space?

   • Furniture: Consider the texture of your furniture pieces. Wooden chairs, silky
   upholstery, or marble tabletops each bring their unique tactile and visual qualities,
   amplifying the vibration of the space.

   • Textile Materials: Decorative pillows, linens, and curtains are excellent opportunities
   to introduce various textile textures that can enhance comfort, performance, and
   aesthetics.

   • Decorative Items: Don’t overlook the potential of decorative items such as vases,
   baskets, art, trinkets, or even plants and floral arrangements to introduce texture and
   visual interest.

   • Floor and Wall Coverings: Floor coverings, like a strategically placed area rug, or
   wall coverings with textured designs, can significantly impact the room’s overall texture
   and energy.

   • Window Treatments: Window treatments go beyond drapes and curtains. They
   include blinds, shutters, Roman shades, roller shades, and woven shades.

   • Architecture: If your home has features like crown molding, chair rails, or coffered
   ceilings, these architectural elements can serve as focal points to showcase texture.

   DON’T RUB ME THE WRONG WAY

   It may not be very sexy, but when we talk about bringing texture into your space,
   we have to have a conversation about durability. I don’t want you to go through this
   process and end up with something that doesn’t actually align with the way you live.

   The right textures not only enhance the way your space looks and elevates the
   energy, but also ensures longevity and ease of maintenance and care. Because as
   always, we want to use the KISS method: Keep It Simple, Superstar!

   Here are some things you’re going to want to consider before choosing textures to
   incorporate into your home:

   • Lifestyle: During this process, make sure to check in with yourself and be real about
   your lifestyle and your special little quirks! Don’t endure the frustration of having textures
   that demand more effort than they are worth. If your household is buzzing with children
   or pets, prioritize durability and easy maintenance to accommodate your daily activities.

   • Location and Purpose: Assess the specific location and purpose of the textured
   elements. In high-traffic areas like the living room or family room where people frequently
   gather, opt for durable fabrics and finishes that can withstand regular use. If you are
   adding texture to an active zone and have kids, you probably don’t want to use any
   materials that are too delicate or precious.

   • Material Selection: If you’re going to be tough on your textures, consider materials
   like microfiber or performance fabrics that resist stains and wear. Natural fibers like
   cotton or linen can be good choices, too.

   • Texture Finish: Pay attention to the finish of your textures. While plush and velvety
   textures may be visually appealing, they can be more susceptible to wear and compression
   over time. Smooth and glossy finishes like glass can show scratches, smudges, and wear
   more easily. If this might be an issue for you, consider textures with a subtle pattern or
   weave that can better conceal signs of use. Textures with a matte finish (like a honed
   countertop) are often more forgiving in terms of wear and can maintain their appearance
   longer. However, it’s important to note that when it comes to paint, the opposite is often
   true, and we’ll delve into that in the next Key!

   • Resistance to Stains: In homes with children or pets, prioritize soft textures that are
   resistant to stains and spills. Fabrics treated with stain-resistant finishes or those labeled
   as easy to clean can be a practical choice.

   • Color and Pattern: Sometimes patterned textures or darker colors can play a role in
   hiding wear and stains on certain textures. They can camouflage minor imperfections
   and make maintenance more manageable.

   • Maintenance: Consider the maintenance requirements of textures. Some materials
   may require regular cleaning or special care to maintain their appearance. Be realistic
   about your willingness to invest time and effort in upkeep when making your selections.
