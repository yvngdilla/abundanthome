                  GET IN THE ZONE

  The next step is to identify your “active” and “passive” zones. To create a high-vibe,
  fully aligned home, it’s crucial to grasp how your current living space functions. You
  need to identify what works, and what doesn’t. Understanding the difference between
  active and passive zones and using this knowledge to organize your belongings
  in alignment with your Newlife Vision will make all the difference in your ability to
  manifest your desires.




  ACTIVE ZONES:
  Active zones refer to the areas in your home that receive frequent interaction and
  usage throughout your daily routines. These spaces are central to your everyday life
  and deserve special attention when it comes to organization. By identifying items
  you frequently access and placing them in active zones, you’ll promote ease of use,
  and prevent clutter at the same time.

  Examples of common active zones include:

          Kitchen countertop
          Entryway or foyer
          Study or home office desk
          Living room coffee table
          Bathroom vanity
          Children’s play area
          Laundry area
          Home gym
          Pet care station

                             Keep in mind
   What is considered active for one household might be perceived as passive for
   another. For instance, if your lifestyle revolves around sports and physical activities,
   your garage may be bustling and packed with items you use daily – while someone
   else might use their garage primarily for long-term storage.

   If you have kids or go to a lot of social events, you may want to store your gift wrapping

    THINGS TO THINK ABOUT:
   WHAT IS A DROP ZONE AND HOW DO I FIND IT?
   A drop zone is like a designated safe haven for items that you bring into your home. This
   space might be the permanent resting place for things like keys or sunglasses, or could
   be a temporary transition zone for things like mail, dog leashes, and other things that will
   ultimately be stored somewhere else.

   For maximum efficiency, you’ll want to strategically position these drop zones within
   active areas, such as the entryway – making it a breeze to deposit items as you enter
   or leave the house. Pay close attention to your natural habits when you come home
   and take note of where you instinctively place your daily essentials upon arrival. Don’t
   try to reinvent the wheel. Once you identify these hotspots, creating designated drop
   zones becomes a simple and effective solution to keep your home tidy, clutter free, and
   effortlessly organized.




  TO SEE OR NOT TO SEE - THAT IS THE QUESTION!
  First off, consider whether it’s necessary or helpful to see certain items (re: keys or
  sunglasses) immediately upon entering the house. If not, explore options to minimize
  visual clutter by concealing or organizing them in a tidy manner at your drop zone.

  Open storage has become super trendy lately, but it’s often more of a display than
  actual storage. Look, I’m all about using your belongings in meaningful ways, so it’s really
  important to consider whether you truly want to have something on display or not.

  Also, keep in mind that whatever you decide to display will affect the energy of your
  space, so think about the significance and impact of each item. For example, if you’re
  focused on manifesting money, don’t hide those precious objects that symbolize wealth
  and prosperity for you! If you’re manifesting love, keep the pretty perfume bottle on the
  bathroom counter to remind you daily of the love that’s on its way.

  By putting these meaningful items in the spotlight, you’re not just decorating and
  organizing your home – you’re igniting the energy of manifestation and drawing your
  desires ever closer to you.

    HOW TO STORE PHOTOGRAPHS
           & ARTWORK:
  Photographs, artwork, and paper memorabilia deserve special treatment. Don’t just
  shove them anywhere! Make sure to store them in acid-free and archival-quality
  storage solutions, such as photo albums, sleeves, or boxes. Acidic materials can
  deteriorate the images and artwork over time. Also, keep your photos and artwork
  away from direct sunlight and harsh UV rays. UV exposure can cause fading and
  discoloration, so store them in light-resistant containers.

  You’ll also want to maintain a stable environment with controlled temperature and
  humidity   levels. Avoid storing precious photos and artwork in basements, attics, or
    @canyoncoffee
  damp areas, as moisture can lead to mold and warping.

               HOW TO STORE FABRICS
                    & TEXTILES:
  Grab yourself some breathable storage bags made from cotton or muslin instead
  of plastic. These allow for air circulation, reducing the risk of mold and mildew –
  keeping your fabrics free from any potential damage. Before storing any fabric,
  make sure it’s clean and completely dry. Any lingering dirt or moisture can lead to
  mold and deterioration over time. You’ll also want to make sure you store them out
  of direct sunlight, in a cool, dry place with stable temperature and humidity levels.
  Fluctuations can harm the fibers and lead to more deterioration. For really deli-
  cate fabrics like silk or vintage textiles, wrap them in acid-free tissue paper. This
  will protect them from any potential chemical reactions and will prevent yellowing.
  Finally, for larger fabrics like quilts or rugs, consider rolling them instead of folding
  to avoid permanent creases and stress on the fibers.

         UNDERUTILIZED AREAS:
   These areas hold untapped potential, offering the perfect opportunity to create
   smart and stylish storage solutions. Utilize vertical spaces, such as empty walls, by
   installing shelves, floating bookcases, or wall-mounted cubbies (just remember
   our conversation about open storage above). Awkward corners or tight spaces
   can become your storage heroes with the addition of corner shelves, slide-out
   racks, or custom cabinets. Transforming the space beneath the stairs into a stor-
   age area with pull-out drawers or custom shelves is also an excellent way to make
   the most of overlooked areas. And don’t forget about utilizing the back of doors
   with over-the-door organizers or hooks for a simple yet effective way to add extra
   storage.




             CORD MANAGEMENT:
   Tangled cords can be an eyesore and lead to a disorganized mess. Invest in cord
   clips and ties to keep cables organized and in place. These handy accessories
   can be attached to the edge of your desk or along the wall to neatly secure and
   guide the cords, preventing them from sprawling all over the place. Also consider
   using cord boxes or organizers to store and conceal power strips and cables when
   you’re charging and powering your devices. These cool little boxes come with
   openings for cords, allowing you to keep them hidden and out of sight while main-
   taining accessibility. If you are willing to spend a little bit more, you could install a
   powered cabinet drawer. Things like hair appliances and phone chargers can stay
   plugged in and ready to go when they are needed – and the cherry on top is that
   the cords stay out of sight!

                      GROUP BY TASK:
   One powerful strategy to make storage easy and efficient is all about strategic
   organization and grouping items by task. Imagine stepping into a space where all
   your morning essentials are neatly gathered in one spot, ready to kickstart your

             STORE HEAVY ITEMS
                DOWN LOW:
   The placement of your items is pretty important when you’re storing your stuff. It
   may seem like a no-brainer, but remember to store heavier items down low. Bal-
   ancing on a stepladder while attempting to lift something heavy is a risky endeav-
   or that you want to avoid if possible. To ensure safety and prevent unnecessary
   strain, keep heavy items at or below waist height. Whether you’re moving boxes,
   maneuvering hefty kitchen equipment like dutch ovens and stand mixers, or deal-
   ing with anything that requires considerable effort to lift, maintaining a waist-level
   placement is definitely the way to go. When you reach for heavy items that are
   too low, you also run the risk of injuring yourself. If you’re thinking about a kitchen
   remodel, there are tons of storage solutions that you can seamlessly incorporate
   into your cabinetry. With these clever additions, storing heavier items and equip-
   ment becomes a breeze, ensuring easy access without the need to physically
   move pieces when you need them.

   Prioritizing safety and proper positioning for heavy items guarantees a well-orga-
   nized and hazard-free environment for everyone.

                                    KITCHEN
   Ok, let’s move on to what is for sure one of the most challenging areas for creating
   mindful storage: the kitchen.

   The kitchen is often one of the most heavily used spaces in a home, leading to a
   continuous influx of groceries, cookware, and utensils that need proper placement.
   With so many items to manage as well as our fast-paced modern lifestyles and
   daily demands, it’s easy for clutter to accumulate quickly (and overwhelm most of
   the available storage space), causing chaos in the heart of the home.

   So let’s break down the space.

   1. Countertops:
   Generally, kitchen countertops are one of the most active open spaces in our
   homes. This is often where we might drop things like mail, keys, school books,
   and dirty dishes. It’s super easy to have a messy kitchen, especially if you have an
   active family or housemates. Even as someone who lives alone, my kitchen counter
   looks like I’m having a yard sale half of the time!
   So to help manage countertop space:

            KEEP IT ESSENTIAL: Again, I’m not trying to get you to be a minimalist if
            that’s not your thing. BUT, if you’re struggling in this area, try to limit the
            number of items you keep on the countertop to essential appliances and
            frequently used items. This will create a clean and spacious work area.

            In my life, tea is essential. I love my teapot and tea cozy. I love my spoon

   2. Kitchen Cabinets/Drawers/Pantry:

   overwhelming and can quickly lead to clutter and disorganization – especially if
   you’re living in a small space. Let me tell you, I can relate. There was a time when I
   had to store my pots and pans in the oven because of a lack of space. It’s a creative
   solution, but it’s not the most efficient, especially if you like to cook.

   When pots and pans stack up in a jumble, and finding the right dish or utensil
   becomes a time-consuming task, it’s super easy to get frustrated, say “the hell with
   this!” and order take out instead. If this is you, here are a few simple tweaks that you
   can try in your home to create a more organized and efficient kitchen space:

            Cabinets and Drawers:

            1. Drawer Organizers: Invest in various-sized drawer organizers to sort and
            keep utensils, measuring spoons, and other kitchen gadgets in order. If
            you store your plates and bowls in your drawers, add pegs and other dish
            dividers for secure and easy access.

            2. Lazy Susans: Utilize or install Lazy Susans in deep cabinets or corner
            spaces to make it simpler to access spices, condiments, or canned goods
            without having to reach to the back. They even have rectangular ones now
            (which is a more efficient use of space) that swing out, making things even
            easier.

            3. Lot Lid Organizers: If you don’t have space to keep your lids on your
            pots, install pot lid organizers on cabinet doors to keep lids neatly in place
            and prevent them from cluttering the cabinets.

            4. Magnetic Strips: Install magnetic strips on the kitchen wall or backsplash
            to hold metal cooking tools like knives, whisks, and spatulas, keeping them
            easily accessible and out of the way.

            5. Under-Shelf Baskets: Attach under-shelf baskets to maximize vertical
            space in cabinets. They are perfect for storing lightweight items like kitchen
            towels, wraps, and cutting boards.
            Vertical Bakeware Organizer: Use space saving vertical bakeware organizers
            to store baking sheets, muffin tins, and cutting boards in an upright position,
            making them easily accessible.

            6. Spice Drawer Liners: Make your jarred spices easy to access and close
            at hand while cooking if you have a drawer next to your oven. These liners
            are great because all your spices are laid out flatley, which makes labels
            super easy to read. .

            Pantry:

            1. Categorize Items and Create Zones: Group similar items together, such
            as canned goods, pasta, baking essentials, snacks, etc. Keep them in
            designated areas that make sense to you and your household.

            2. Label Everything: If (and that’s a big if!) you decide to transfer goods
            into new containers, use adhesive labels or a label maker to identify each
            one. You think you’ll remember the difference between your pearled barley
            and pearled couscous, but I can almost guarantee that you won’t -- so just
            label it!

            3. Optimize Shelf Space: Use adjustable shelves or shelf dividers to
            customize your pantry layout. This can be a good option for things like
            spices and cans and ensures you can fit items of various sizes without
            wasting any space.

            4. Drawer or Pull-Out Solutions: If possible, include pull-out drawers or
            sliding shelves to access items at the back of deep shelves with ease.
            These are great to add if you’re remodeling your kitchen with new cabinets,
            but there are also after-market solutions that you can install on your own
            as well.

            5. Keep Frequently Used Items Accessible: There’s nothing more irritating
            than having to shuffle a bunch of cans and bottles around just to get to
            an item you want. So, make it make sense and store everyday essentials so
            they are easy to access. It may sound a little silly, but if you only use the
            truffle salt a couple of times a year, store it in the back and place your garlic
            powder work-horse front and center.

               LIVING ROOM/FAMILY ROOM:
   1. Living Room Drop Zone: If you don’t have a foyer or traditional entryway, this is
   a great place for a temporary drop zone for things like toys, bags, and mail. Use
   baskets or decorative boxes to temporarily house objects, until you are ready to
   take them to their final destination.

   2. Functional Furniture: If you’re short on space, choose furniture pieces with built-
   in storage compartments, like coffee tables with shelves or ottomans with hidden
   storage to conceal items while enhancing the room’s design.

   3. Multi-Use Shelving: As I mentioned before, open shelves typically function more
   like display than storage. But in the living room, they are great to display cherished
   décor pieces, books, and mementos. If you have things to hide like TV remotes or
   mail, use storage bins or baskets that stylishly hide clutter.
   4. Bookcases, Built-Ins and Cabinets: Custom-built cabinets or entertainment
   centers can ingeniously conceal media equipment, board games, movies, and
   music.

   5. Strategic Zones: Divide your room into designated zones based on activities.
   Each zone can incorporate storage that aligns with its purpose, from entertainment
   centers to workspaces.

   6. Baskets and Storage Trunks: Use these as both decor and functional hideaways.
   In them, you can stash away extra blankets, pillows, toys, or seasonal items.

   7. Cable Management: As I mentioned before, proper cable management is crucial
   in the living room area because this is where we typically have a lot of cables
   and electronics. Use cable organizers, boxes, and baskets to seamlessly manage
   cords and wires. Concealing cables will create a serene environment that doesn’t
   sacrifice your technology’s functionality.

   8. Media Storage: Arrange media units with thoughtfulness. Use dedicated shelves
   or drawers to keep DVDs, remotes, and gaming consoles organized, ensuring
   entertainment without the clutter.

   9. Family-Friendly Solutions: Use open bins or baskets that encourage kids to stow
   away their toys after playtime. These areas are great for pet items, too. You might still
   see the items, but at least they will be contained, instead of strewn all over your floor.
