  WHAT’S THE PLAN, NAN?!?

  So, what is a floor plan, anyway? A floor plan is a flat 2D drawing of a structure or
  space that you look at from a top view. You can think about floor plans by imagining
  looking down on your home from the ceiling with the roof removed.

  A floor plan is like a detailed map that shows all the rooms, walls, doors, windows,
  and important details in your space without the distractions of design elements like
  color, pattern, or texture. It’s a great tool for visualization, space efficiency, directing
  traffic flow, and creating balance in your design.

  I think we’ve all heard the phrase, “form follows function,” right? What that means is
  that things or objects are created because they provide a functional solution. This
  is true whether we are talking about a house or a corkscrew. It’s sort of another way
  of saying, “Necessity is the mother of invention.”

                     THE SPACE BETWEEN

   If you’re getting nervous about this next more technical step - don’t worry! I’m not
   asking you to become a draftsman. You might have an image of someone doing
   precise measurements and precision drawings, but the reality is I always do just a
   rough freehand sketch first.

   Next, I measure the space. Then I go back and make any adjustments to my sketch
   that seem, well…frankly, a bit “sketchy.”

   Here are the basics about how to measure your space like a pro! Keep in mind you
   don’t have to do all of this. Use what makes sense to you! Whoo Hoo!

   YOU’LL NEED YOUR DRAWING TOOLS:

         • A measuring tape / digital measuring tool
         • Straight Edge / Ruler / Scale (optional)
         • Paper / Graph paper
         • Pencil / Eraser
         • Phone/Camera

   Nothing has to be to scale, and general measurements will do at this point. It’s a
   sketch on paper or on your computer; it can always be revised :o)

         1. Draw the Exterior / Perimeter Walls: Start by drawing the exterior, or
         perimeter walls and label them. INCLUDE THE DRIVEWAY IF YOU HAVE ONE!

         2. Draw the Interior Walls that divide the space into rooms and areas. Label
         the rooms and add your measurements. Note the height of the ceilings.

         3. Draw the Openings, Doors, Windows, etc.: Measure the space around the
         openings so you can accurately place them in the walls. Label these features
         w/ their dimensions of their location: width, height & depth.

  TMI - WHEN TOO MUCH INFORMATION
         CAN BE A GOOD THING!

   Most of us cringe when we get TMI, but in this case, information is your friend. Using
   a scale can be really helpful when creating a floor plan - if you know how to use one.

   When working on home projects, interior designers will typically use a ¼ inch scale
   (¼ inch on paper = 1 foot in real life). However, if you don’t have a scale, don’t sweat
   it - sketching a rough floor plan is still very useful to help you understand your space.

   Personally, I skip the scale and use graph paper when I’m creating a floor plan. The
   grid helps to keep lines straighter and you can also use the grid lines to roughly
   measure. It’s a great cheat sheet!

   For example, each square within your graph paper can equal one foot. (1 sq = 1 ft) If
   your room is 20 ft x14 ft, just count 20 squares for your horizontal line (the width of the
   room), then draw a vertical line perpendicular to your horizontal line 14 squares up (or
   down) for the length of the room.

   Then, if you’re dealing with a room that is just a rectangle, all you have to do is connect
   the last two lines. Most homes are a series of rectangles and squares, with connecting
   hallways, so you see how easy this can be, tweedledee!

   POSITIONING YOUR PORTALS: Once you’ve completed your basic floor plan,
   you’ll have your boundaries (your walls) in place. Then, you’ll need to draw in the
   access points, or “portals.” Portals are a way to let energy in and out! Examples are
   doors, windows, or any other openings, like an archway.

             • Add Doors and Windows: Draw two perpendicular lines with the
             approximate width of the door or between them (like goal posts). If you’re
             not using a scale, just eyeball where you think they are.

             • Note the measurements on either side: Let’s say your wall is 15 ft long
             and your window is 3 feet in from the left corner. You would mark 3ft from
             the left corner to the window, then mark the width of the window - let’s say
             it’s 2ft. That means the space on the right side of the window is 10ft.

              Most doors are between 32”-36” wide, 6’ 8” high.

              We are not doing elevations (3D drawings) so just make a note of the
              space at the top and bottom (for windows) if applicable.

              • Draw the swing: The door swing shows the direction which the door
              opens. Keep in mind when the door is open it’s still 36”. So the “arch” of
              the door takes up space as it’s opening (and on the wall when it’s fully
              open, too!).

              Depending on which way the door swings is where you place the arch:
              either to the left or the right. Note whether it opens into or out of the
              room. Is it a push, or a pull? They swing both ways, sista!

              Most windows don’t swing open, but if they do, you need to note that as
              well. You don’t want to put the tall wingback chair in front of an inward-
              swinging window!

   These notes. will really come in handy when you’re moving your furniture in and out.
   It’s no fun when you lug the sectional up the stairs to find out it doesn’t fit through
   the door. UGH!

      YOU’VE GOT THE LOOK -
    AESTHETICS & DESIGN STYLE
   Your home’s architectural style provides a foundation, and it’s often a good starting
   point for your interior design choices. It’s like having a theme that guides your decisions,
   ensuring that everything is balanced and flows seamlessly.

   However, there’s room for creative freedom even within the context of your home’s
   architecture. If you happen to dislike the style of your home’s exterior, you don’t
   necessarily have to mirror it inside. What’s important is that there’s a sense of relationship
   or intentional connection between the two realms.

   If you’ve had the pleasure to travel throughout Europe, you might have noticed century-
   old homes that have super modern interiors.

   It works beautifully because it’s done with intention. The key is creating a sense of
   connection, flow, and balance - even if it’s through contrast.

   I share all of this to reassure you that you don’t have to be bound by your home’s
   architectural style. BUT, you should aim for a thoughtful design that bridges the gap
   and ensures a sense of cohesiveness throughout your space. It’s all about making your
   space uniquely yours by striking a balance that resonates with you and ensuring that it
   all flows together, whether that’s through harmony or thoughtful contrast.

   THE GOLDEN RULE

   We all know the Golden Rule, “Do unto others as you would want done unto you.”
   But have you heard of the Golden Ratio rule of 2:3 ratio?

   The interior design golden rule of a 2:3 ratio is a guideline that helps create
   balanced and visually pleasing spaces. In simple terms, it means that when you’re
   decorating a room, you should aim to have two-thirds of one element and one-
   third of another element. We’ll be using this 2:3 ratio throughout the course, in
   lighting, paint, patterns, window treatments, artwork and accessories!

   It’s not an exact science, so just like with your floor plan creation, don’t worry
   about perfection! Use your “eye” to balance the room, the objects and the light
   with a basic 2:3 ratio. YOU’VE GOT THIS!

   When arranging furniture, you can apply the 2:3 ratio by placing larger furniture
   items (like a sofa or a bed) in two-thirds of the space and smaller furniture or
   decorative elements in the remaining one-third. This helps create a sense of
   proportion and balance in the room.

   • Symmetrical Layout: Symmetry is a go-to option, compatible with rooms of all sizes
   and shapes. It’s a key element for achieving visual harmony. Imagine two sofas elegantly
   facing each other, with a table in between; this classic setup defines a symmetrical layout.
   Another symmetrical choice is a sofa flanked by matching side chairs, where balance
   reigns supreme.

   • Floating Layout: While pushing furniture against walls might seem like a space-
   saving strategy, it can actually lead to an impersonal and unwelcoming atmosphere. This
   arrangement often lacks cozy zones, visual interest, and can result in unused dead space.
   It disrupts the balance and functionality of a room. If you have the space, here’s where
   a floating layout comes to the rescue. Position furniture a few feet away from the walls,
   forming an “island” in the room’s center. For functionality, mount a TV on one of the walls.
   Placing a console table or chest behind the sofa can look beautiful and help to ground
   the arrangement.

   • L-shaped Layout: L-shaped living room and dining room combinations can be tricky
   due to limited wall space for furniture and TV placement. Don’t sweat it. Typically, two
   walls serve as the canvas for arranging your furniture and positioning the TV. Visualize
   the sofa resting against the longer wall, with the TV aligned on the opposite wall directly
   facing the sofa.

   • Layouts for Irregular Rooms: When faced with the quirkiness of narrow living spaces,
   nooks, crannies, or rooms featuring angled fireplaces, it’s time for creative solutions. The
   key lies in segmenting the room using furniture placed atop area rugs to define distinct
   zones. You can anchor a zone with an attention-grabbing piece, such as a dramatic floor
   lamp.

            recommended minimum clearance of 36 inches for main traffic areas.

            Door and Window Clearances: Ensure that doors and windows can

            clearance in front of doors for easy access.
