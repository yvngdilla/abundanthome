   Balance - In design, balance revolves around the distribution of visual weight
   within a space, and it plays a vital role in creating a stable and harmonious
   room. Designers typically employ two primary types of balance: symmetrical and
   asymmetrical.

        1. Symmetrical balance relies on mirroring elements on both sides of a
   room, imparting a formal and traditional atmosphere.

         2. Asymmetrical balance achieves equilibrium through the juxtaposition of
   contrasting elements like colors, sizes, or textures, which is frequently embraced
   in contemporary and eclectic design approaches.

   Harmony - Harmony revolves around achieving unity and cohesion within a
   space. It requires all design elements to collaborate seamlessly. To establish this
   harmony, your chosen patterns should not only complement other elements but
   also harmonize with other diverse patterns within the space.

   Rhythm - Rhythm is created through the visual repetition of form. By using
   patterns to introduce rhythm, we can direct attention around the space and
   enhance the overall impression of the room.

   Emphasis - The use of an accent in the room - such as color, a furniture piece,
   texture, or a focal point - creates interest and draws attention in a space. A big,
   bold pattern can be used to create emphasis too.

   Contrast - Contrast provides visual interest and helps to define spaces. It can
   be very interesting and beautiful when done correctly, but as a rule, it should
   be used sparingly. Too much contrast can be jarring and overwhelm a space,
   especially when using patterns.

   Every decision you make while creating your custom design will be based on
   these foundational design principles. When we use these principles, everything
   aligns - and we send a clear message to the Universe that cannot be denied!

                    PATTERN POWER PLAY

   Patterns can dramatically impact a space, whether that is on an accent wall, a
   throw pillow, a piece of artwork, or window treatments. There are so many places
   where patterns can be used to make a powerful statement (Don’t forget the
   ceiling!!). WHOO HOO!

   Here are some key aspects of patterns that will help you design your space (just
   like the pros!):

            1. Visual Interest and Variety: Patterns add visual interest and variety
            to a room. They break up the monotony of solid colors and create
            focal points that draw the eye. Without patterns, a space can feel one-
            dimensional and less engaging.
            2. Room Function: Patterns can support the intended function of a
            room. For instance, in a lively, social area like a living room, patterns
            with energy and movement can enhance the pleasant atmosphere. In
            a bedroom, calming and soothing patterns can promote relaxation.
            3. Spatial Illusion: Patterns can also be used to create spatial illusions.
            Vertical stripes can make a room feel taller, while horizontal stripes can
            make it appear wider. This can be particularly helpful in small or oddly
            shaped spaces.
            4. Coordinating Design Elements: Patterns can tie various design
            elements together. They can connect colors, shapes, and styles,
            providing cohesion in a space that might otherwise feel disjointed.


   The next time you’re in a restaurant, hotel lobby, or airport - take a look around
   and notice the different patterns at play. Look at the flooring, the walls, and
   the seating. Hospitality designers often use patterns to make a room feel more
   activated and interesting, but they do so in a way that is very intentional. Patterns
   are often used to guide people throughout the space with a specific purpose.

   Below, you’ll also learn how to mix and match patterns to create an energy flow
   that will give you a deeper understanding of the context and function of the space
   to make patterns work effectively in your interior design and your manifestations.

      THE ART OF PATTERN PLAY
  There are some important strategies to use when designing with patterns. These
  strategies will ensure that you have a gorgeous space that you love - and will help
  your home become a powerful and unstoppable manifestation magnet for everything
  that you want!

  When mixing patterns, keep the following in mind:


   1. Use various sizes: Find a large-scale or statement pattern to build off of first.
   Then, pair your statement pattern with simpler ones. Remember, it’s all about getting
   the balance right, so let your most special “showstopper” pattern really shine by
   complementing it with smaller patterns that are a bit less intricate or intense
   (depending on how much energy you want to create).

   2. Think about placement: Consider which elements will showcase the patterns
   best, like upholstery, curtains, or accent pillows. A large-scale print may not work on
   a smaller-scale item (such as a throw pillow), while a small pattern may get lost on a
   larger item or area.

   3. Too much of a good thing: Mixing too many things at one time can overwhelm a
   room and make it feel disjointed and chaotic. A good rule of thumb is to stick to three
   to four varying patterns - especially if this is all new to you. Also, adding in some solid
   materials (like a plain velvet pillow along with your floral and striped ones) can help
   to break up the pattern play and give your eye some rest.

   4. Add some contrast - Even with a minimal color palette, adding a little contrast to
   your patterns can help to give variety and emphasis to certain patterns. If you love
   beige on beige, mixing in darker and lighter shades or tints of one color can help
   elevate varying patterns and your design.

   5. Trial and Error: Don’t be afraid to experiment. Arrange your patterns and live with
   them for a while. If something feels off, don’t hesitate to make adjustments. Design is
   a process, and it’s okay to tweak until it feels just right.
