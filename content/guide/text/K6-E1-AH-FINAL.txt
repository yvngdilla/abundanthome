   Using pillows and throws is such a fast and easy way to make a significant impact in
   your space. Because they are smaller elements within your design, they really allow
   you to add some extra energy, and allow you to get super-creative in your space by
   doing the least amount of work possible (they might even inspire you to take a risk or
   two - remember my friend with leopard print pillows?).

   One of my favorite things about throws and decorative pillows is their incredible
   versatility. Not only that, but they are returnable, too (unless you’re doing custom
   pillows!),

   I always think of pillows and throws as “jewelry” for your furniture. You can transform
   your simple “black dress” from casually comfortable to classically elegant in seconds
   by changing your jewelry, right? It’s the same with pillows and throws on your furniture!

   Need a cozier atmosphere? Layer in those fluffy textured pillows. Want to add a touch
   of elegance? You’ll want to use a bit more structure to your pillows and access your
   pattern knowledge to tastefully arrange them for that extra dash of panache.

  Pillow and Throw Pro-Tips:

  Always take into consideration the shape, style, and texture of the furniture you’ll be
  placing your pillows and throws upon when making your selections. The furniture is
  the canvas for your masterpiece.

  1. Mix and Match: Don’t limit yourself to one style or pattern. Mixing different pillow
  designs, textures, and sizes can create visual interest. Try combining solids, patterns,
  and textures that complement each other and reflect your desires.

  2. Fold or Drape: Throws can be folded neatly for a tailored look or casually draped
  for an effortless, lived-in appearance. Both styles can work, depending on your
  design preference and what you are wanting to manifest.

  3. Odd Numbers: Symmetry is great, but so is asymmetry! Odd numbered groupings
  of pillows, like three or five, create a balanced yet dynamic look. Odd numbers create
  energy. Even numbers create serenity.

   4. Layer with Purpose: Layering pillows of various sizes and shapes adds depth
   and dimension, which creates energy. Start with larger pillows at the back and layer
   smaller ones in front. Layering throws can instantly add warmth and texture to your
   space. Drape them over sofas, chairs, or the foot of your bed for a cozy and inviting
   atmosphere.

   5. Texture Play: Incorporate pillows and throws with different textures, such as velvet,
   silk, knit, faux fur, or cashmere to create a tactile experience. Refer to your sensory
   chart for textural/emotional connections.

   6. Seasonal Swaps: Change your decorative pillows and throws with the seasons or
   holidays. Lighter, brighter colors for spring and summer, and warmer, cozier options
   for fall and winter. Plan for storage of your seasonal swaps!

   7. Focal Point: Use a standout pillow as a small focal point. It could be a bold color or a
   unique pattern that creates energy and focus!

   8. Size Matters: Choose pillow sizes in proportion to your furniture. Larger sofas can
   handle larger pillows, while smaller chairs benefit from smaller ones.

   9. Pillow Inserts and Covers: Invest in quality pillow inserts. Down or down-alternative
   inserts offer a plush and comfortable look that you can easily switch out without having
   to have loads of pillows around - it also makes it easier to keep your pillows fresh and
   clean! Use a larger insert in a smaller cover (for instance, a 20” x 20” insert in an 18” x 18”
   cover) for a full and robust look.

   10. Consider the Room’s Purpose: Keep in mind the function of the room. For a formal
   living room, go for a more structured and symmetrical arrangement. In a casual space,
   embrace a relaxed and eclectic mix of pillows.

   11. Storage Solution: Use decorative baskets, crates, or storage ottomans to keep
   throws neatly organized and easily accessible when needed. This will help you steer
   clear of clutter :o)

   12. Maintenance: Regularly shake out and gently fluff your throws to keep them looking
   fresh. Follow care instructions to ensure their longevity. Refer to the Fabric section below
   to explore durability factors.

   13. Don’t Overdo It: While decorative pillows can enhance your design, you probably
   don’t need 10 pillows on your bed. So, don’t overcrowd your furniture with too many.
   Leave some space for people to sit comfortably and for your eye to rest.

  HOW TO CHOOSE THE PERFECT RUG

  Choosing the wrong size rug is one of the most frequent mistakes people make in
  design.Take your time and consider these simple principles BEFORE making your
  decision…and keep your receipt in case you make the same mistake I did :o)

  1. Where is Your Rug Going?
  Before starting on your rug journey, consider the destination. Where do you plan
  to place the rug? The location sets the stage for everything. Are you thinking of the
  living room, bedroom, dining area, or hallway? Knowing the room’s purpose and
  traffic patterns will guide your rug selection. The thickness of the rug needs to be
  considered as well. Is it a tripping hazard?

  2. What Look and Feeling Do You Want?
  Rugs have the uncanny ability to evoke specific emotions and feelings in a space.
  So determine the mood you want to create in your home. Are you aiming for comfort
  and relaxation, elegance and tradition, or a modern and versatile vibe? Your desired
  vibration will guide your choice of rug style, pattern, and texture.

  3. How Much Space Do You Have to Work With?
  Measure the area where you intend to place the rug. It’s important to ensure the rug
  fits comfortably within the space, leaving enough room for a border of flooring around
  it. Because a rug that’s too small can leave your room feeling disconnected, and a rug
  that is too big looks awkward and out of place. So, take into account the dimensions
  of your furniture and the layout of the room. Refer to you furniture plan :o)

  4. What’s Your Budget?
  Rugs come in a wide range of price points, so it’s essential to determine how much
  you’re willing to invest. Keep in mind that the rug’s size, material, and craftsmanship
  can affect its cost.

  COMMON RUG STYLES:

  There are plenty of different styles of rugs out there to tickle your design fancy. Here
  is a list of some of my favorites to consider for your space.

  Shag Rugs (Comfort, Coziness, Relaxation)
  Shag rugs, with their long and plush fibers, often evoke feelings of comfort and
  coziness. They are perfect for creating a relaxed and inviting atmosphere, making a
  space feel warm and comforting.

  Persian or Oriental Rugs (Tradition, Elegance, Heritage)
  Persian or Oriental rugs are known for their intricate patterns and rich colors. They
  can evoke a sense of tradition, elegance, and connection to heritage. Incorporating
  these rugs may symbolize a desire for cultural richness and timeless sophistication.

  Flatweave Rugs (Simplicity, Versatility, Modernity)
  Flatweave rugs, such as kilims or dhurries, are characterized by a flat, woven texture.
  They bring a sense of simplicity and versatility to a space, making them suitable
  for modern and eclectic designs. Choosing flatweave rugs may signify a desire for
  simplicity and adaptability in life.

  Cut Pile (Coziness, Ease, Simplicity)
  These rugs are prized for their luxurious comfort, durability, and design versatility.
  Their soft and plush texture makes them a go-to choice for spaces where coziness
  matters, like bedrooms and living rooms. With tightly woven or tufted fibers, they can
  withstand heavy wear-and-tear, making them ideal for high-traffic areas. They’re easy
  to maintain, resist stains, and offer options like plush, Saxony, frieze, and shag piles.

                 MAGIC CARPET RIDE
  “A whole new world…”

  I’m often asked - “Is it okay to put a rug on top of wall-to-wall carpet?”
  Some rail against it, but in my expert opinion, the answer is yes. Here’s why. Whether
  you have a soft or hard surface underfoot, a rug is still the element in the room that
  is going to define, anchor, and pull your space together. Just keep in mind these
  golden rules when you decide to have a little rug-on-rug action: size, position, and
  contrast.

           • Opt for a rug that’s smaller than the room to avoid overwhelming the
           space, leaving a balanced border.

           • Carefully position it under key furniture pieces to define areas or create or
           accentuate focal points.

           • Select a rug with a different texture or pattern to create visual interest.

   Placement is key. It’s all about balance and harmony. Often we see a room with a rug
   placed smack dab in the center of it, almost creating an island in the center of the
   room. This placement is not always necessary (or feasible) depending on the space,
   but you do want to make sure that the ultimate placement makes sense.

   Be mindful of door swings and tripping hazards.

  FABRICS

  If you are going to make a bigger purchase like a piece of upholstered furniture or a rug,
  try to figure those pieces out first, before you move on to smaller, more easily changeable
  items.

  These foundational items lay the groundwork for your design and provide stability, allowing
  you to layer in those delightful decorative touches with purpose and consideration.

  If you’re thinking about adding some upholstered or soft surface pieces to your design,
  here are some fabrics you may want to consider for your space:

  I concentrate more on expensive pieces like upholstery, rugs, and heavy textiles like window
  treatments that you will most likely not switch out frequently. I’ll find fabric that will work with
  my initial color palette and go from there.

  I like to find a “showstopper fabric” or at the very least, one that I feel that I need to incorporate
  into my design. Then I build off of that fabric with coordinating patterns and textures. Once
  I have all of my fabrics and textures dialed in, I’ll either pull colors from those fabrics to pick
  my paint, or use them to find coordinating paint colors that go well with the fabric.

  By beginning with textiles and patterns, you’ll find that you have a more flexible and creative
  starting point.

  First, consider the room’s purpose. Do you need soft and calming fabrics for a bedroom?
  Or perhaps you need durable, stain-resistant options for a busy dining area. Consider your
  fabric choices (cotton, linen, leather, silk, etc.), then revisit the patterns and textures that
  resonate with your personal design style and manifestation goals.

  Here are some common fabric attributes for you to consider. Don’t limit yourself! I’ve used
  Sunbrella fabrics in the living room with great success! Have fun, but be mindful of your
  ultimate goal of manifesting in beautiful ways.

  • Cotton: Ideal for living room sofas, armchairs, and throw pillows. Cotton’s comfort makes
  it suitable for casual, everyday use. Cotton fabrics create a cozy and inviting atmosphere.

  • Linen: Works great in spaces like living rooms, bedrooms, or sunrooms. Linen works well
  on slipcovers, draperies, shower curtains, and decorative cushions. Airy, fresh, and casual.
  Linen upholstery imparts a sense of lightness and informality.

  • Velvet: Luxurious, opulent, and elegant. Perfect for elegant living rooms, master bedrooms,
  curtains, or dining chairs. Velvet adds a touch of luxury to furniture and headboards. Velvet
  can evoke feelings of richness and sophistication.

  • Leather: Commonly used on sofas, recliners, and occasional chairs. Leather adds a
  classic touch and works well in masculine or traditional settings. Leather creates a timeless,
  rugged, and classic atmosphere. Leather upholstery exudes durability and timeless appeal.

  • Microfiber: Practical, family-friendly, and low-maintenance. Works well in family rooms,
  playrooms, and home theaters due to its durability and ease of cleaning. Microfiber provides
  a sense of ease and functionality.

  • Wool: Warm, cozy, and natural. Suited for rugs, throws,upholstering dining chairs, and
  mid-century modern furniture. Wool exudes warmth and a tactile feel. Woolen fabrics can
  offer comfort and a sense of well-being.

  • Silk: Glamorous, refined, and elegant. Usually reserved for more formal spaces like dining
  rooms or boudoirs. Silk can be used on accent pieces, bed linens, or draperies. Silk adds a
  touch of luxury and opulence to any space.

  • Polyester: Durable, budget-friendly, and easy-care. Versatile and affordable, polyester
  can be used on a wide range of furniture, including sofas, loveseats, and accent chairs.
  Polyester imparts a sense of practicality and affordability.

  • Chenille: Plush, soft, and tactile. Living rooms and bedrooms can benefit from the
  plushness of chenille on sofas, ottomans, and decorative cushions. Chenille upholstery
  invites touch and relaxation.

  • Faux Fur: Cozy, playful, and luxurious. Adds a touch of coziness to bedrooms, living
  rooms, reading nooks, or accent chairs. Faux fur adds warmth and a sense of indulgence.

  • Bouclé: Modern, textured, and sophisticated. Ideal for modern or minimalist designs,
  often used on iconic mid-century modern chairs and sofas. Bouclé creates a contemporary
  and tactile feel.

  • Canvas: Casual, versatile, and rugged. Durable and casual, canvas works well for
  slipcovers, poufs, curtains, and accent pillows. Canvas imparts a sense of comfort and laid-
  back charm.

  • Jacquard: Elegant, ornate, and decorative. Elevates formal dining room chairs, curtains,
  or decorative cushions. Jacquard fabrics elicit feelings of opulence and intricate design.

  • Crypton: Practical, worry-free, and low-stress. High-traffic areas like family rooms,
  children’s playrooms, or pet-friendly spaces benefit from Crypton’s durability. Crypton
  fabrics offer peace of mind and easy maintenance.

  • Sunbrella: Resilient, outdoor-ready, and vibrant. Perfect for outdoor furniture such as
  patio sets, cushions, and even outdoor drapes. Sunbrella fabrics bring vitality and durability
  to outdoor living spaces.

  PRO TIP: If you’re ordering new furniture, be sure and get fabric samples before making
  any final decisions, to see how they interact with your existing decor and lighting conditions.
  Put all the fabrics that interest you together to help find the right balance between patterns
  and solid colors. Keep in mind what you’ve learned about scale to create an engaging yet
  harmonious design.

  Remember that fabric trends come and go, so choose options that genuinely resonate with
  your long-term Newlife Vision.
