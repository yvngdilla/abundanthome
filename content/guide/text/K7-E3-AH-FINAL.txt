        TREE HUGGING, FOREST BATHING,

    donnamoderna.com



   There is a new heightened awareness lately about the real benefits of being
   in the forest. While the terms forest bathing and forest therapy are often used
   interchangeably, forest bathing refers to an actual mobile meditation (meant to be
   experienced under a canopy of trees) that was prescribed in Japan during the 1980s
   as a part of its national health program.

   Forest therapy is not just a walk in the woods, however. It’s a conscious and
   contemplative practice of being immersed in the sights, sounds, and smells of
   the forest. Those who practice forest therapy are guided through a quiet, natural
   experience during which they are asked to sequentially focus on the various senses.

   Research has consistently shown that forest therapy improves depression, anxiety,
   and self-esteem, as well as many other symptoms. Over the years, researchers have
   also found improvements in blood pressure, pulse, heart rate variability, and even
   stress hormone levels in saliva. According to several studies, spending time in nature
   improves nearly every mental health parameter.

   This therapeutic essence applies to all plants found within nature. Even the smallest
   plant breathes life and joy into the world. So it’s no surprise that biophilic design

    CHOOSING PLANTS MINDFULLY
   • Think about What You Want to Manifest: If you want to manifest a soft and easy
   life, you probably don’t want to bring in a bunch of harsh-looking (and feeling) plants
   or flowers into your home.

   • Consider the Light Requirements: Before choosing plants, observe the light
   conditions in your home. Different plants require different light levels, so it’s important
   to match the plant to the available light source to ensure its health and growth. For
   example, succulents and cacti thrive in bright light, while ferns and snake plants can
   do well in lower-light conditions.

   • Think About Maintenance: Be realistic about the time and effort you can dedicate
   to plant care. Some plants require more attention than others. If you have a busy
   lifestyle, opt for low-maintenance plants like ZZ plants, pothos, or philodendrons.

   • Create Style Harmony: Choose plants that complement your interior design.
   Consider the color, texture, and size of the plants in relation to your space and decor.
   For instance, a large fiddle leaf fig might suit a spacious, well-lit living room, while
   smaller potted herbs could be perfect for a kitchen windowsill.

   • Use the Right Containers: Plant pots and containers are an integral part of your
   decor. Select pots that not only suit the needs of the plants (like drainage), but also
   align with your interior design style. Think about the material, color, and the design of
   the pots.

   • Vary Plant Heights: Create visual interest by having plants at different heights. Use
   stands, shelves, or hanging planters to achieve varied levels. This can add depth and
   dimension to your space.

   • Group Plants Mindfully: Consider grouping plants together to create a mini indoor
   garden effect. Plants with similar care requirements can be placed together for ease
   of maintenance, and the grouping can make a more impactful visual statement.

   • Balance with Space: While plants can enhance a room, too many can make a
   space feel cluttered. Strike a balance by placing them strategically and allowing for
   plenty of open space.

   • Mind the Health Benefits: Recognize the benefits of plants beyond aesthetics.
   They can improve your mood, reduce stress, and increase creativity and productivity.
   Place plants in areas where you spend a lot of time, like near your workstation or in
   your living area.

   • Rotate Your Plants: If you want a full look (or if your plants aren’t placed up against
   a wall), occasionally rotate your plants to ensure they maintain their shape and
   receive even light. This also gives you a chance to check their health and notice
   any issues.

   • Safety: Many common houseplants, outdoor plants, and flowers are toxic if
   ingested, which can pose a risk to pets and small children. Make sure to research
   and select plants that are safe for your home environment. For instance, while
   peace lilies and snake plants are popular, they’re toxic to pets.

  If you want to incorporate and style with plants, here are some ways to display them
  effectively, without sacrificing space:

  • Tall Plants With Corner Stands or Tables: Revitalize those tricky, empty corners
  and awkward spaces by introducing tall plants, plant stands or tables. Not only do
  they breathe life into unused areas, but they also help to guide the eye through the
  room, creating a more cohesive and inviting space. Just be mindful to match the
  plant’s needs with the spot you choose; a sun-seeking plant won’t thrive in a dark
  corner.

  • Wall-Mounted/Floating Shelves: Install shelves specifically for your plants. This
  not only keeps them out of the way but can also create an attractive living wall effect.
  It’s great for small spaces or for adding greenery without sacrificing floor space.

  • Mantels and Bookcases: Incorporating plants into mantels or bookshelves, along
  with other decorative items, introduces a vibrant touch of greenery, creating an extra
  layer of visual interest to your styled arrangements. This approach smartly utilizes
  vertical spaces, helping to keep your floors and counters uncluttered and open.

  • Narrow Console Tables: Placing a narrow table behind a sofa, at the entry, or
  along a hallway with plants on it can add greenery without obstructing movement.

  • Window Sills: Utilize the natural light from your windows by placing plants on the
  window sills. This is perfect for small plants like herbs or succulents.

  • Tiered Plant Stands: These stands allow you to display multiple plants in a small
  footprint, stacking them vertically.

  • Hanging Planters: Maximize your home’s vertical space by suspending plants from
  ceiling hooks or wall brackets, adding a dynamic layer to your decor. However, tread
  carefully with this method to avoid a Swiss-cheese effect on your ceiling or walls with
  unwanted holes should you choose to remove them later.

  If I had a dollar for every time someone told me, “I can’t keep plants alive!” Well, let’s
  just say I’d have a lot of dollars…

  So, for those who feel less confident in their green thumb abilities, I’m going to share
  a few suggestions for plants that have a really good chance of surviving, under
  anyone’s care! These plants are hardy, easy to care for, tend to be more forgiving of
  neglect, and can thrive with minimal attention. We love that, woo hoo!

  1. Snake Plant (Sansevieria): Snake plants are very resilient and can survive with low
      light and water. They’re known for their striking, upright leaves and air-purifying
      qualities. Water sparingly, as they can easily rot if overwatered.
  2. ZZ Plant (Zamioculcas Zamiifolia): The ZZ plant is another low-maintenance
      option that can thrive in low light conditions and requires infrequent watering. Its
      waxy, green leaves can add a vibrant touch to any space.
  3. Pothos (Epipremnum Aureum): Pothos is a hardy vine with heart-shaped leaves
      that can tolerate a variety of lighting conditions, including low light. It’s very
      forgiving if you forget to water it now and then.
  4. Spider Plant (Chlorophytum Comosum): Spider plants are easy to care for and
      can thrive in a variety of conditions. They prefer bright, indirect light but can also
      adapt to lower light levels. They’re also known for producing baby spider plants
      that can be easily propagated.
  5. Philodendron: With its heart-shaped leaves and vining habit, philodendrons bring
      a lush, tropical feel to any space. They’re adaptable to various light conditions
      and prefer the soil to dry out between waterings.
  6. Aglaonema: Known for its strikingly patterned leaves, Aglaonema thrives in low
      light and needs only occasional watering, making it a stylish and resilient choice
      for indoor gardeners.
  7. Scindapsus (also known as Silver Pothos): This vining plant features beautiful,
      silver-flecked leaves and thrives in moderate to low light. It’s forgiving of irregular
      watering, growing well with just a bit of attention.
  8. Rubber Plant (Ficus Elastica): The rubber plant is a durable plant that prefers
      indirect light and only needs to be watered when the soil is dry to the touch. It’s
      known for its large, glossy leaves.
  9. Aloe Vera: Aloe vera is a succulent that thrives on neglect, requiring minimal
      watering. It prefers bright, indirect sunlight and is also known for its medicinal
      properties.
  10. Cast Iron Plant (Aspidistra Elatior): The name is not a coincidence. The cast iron
      plant is extremely tough and can thrive in low light and with minimal watering. It’s
      known for its ability to survive in less than ideal conditions.
  11. Haworthia: This small succulent is easy to care for and does well in bright, indirect
      light or partial shade. It requires minimal watering and can tolerate neglect.

  For those who love the idea of real plants but find their care daunting, there are
  fantastic apps available that demystify the process, making a green-thumb gardener
  out of just about anyone. But, if the thought of nurturing living plants still seems like
  a bridge too far, faux greenery can offer a beautiful alternative.

  Faux-plants can bring the same sense of nature’s tranquility and beauty into your
  space without the maintenance that real plants require. The key to making faux plants
  look convincingly real lies in their placement, quality, and a bit of creativity.

  It may be a bit more money, but I suggest you opt for higher-quality faux-flora that has
  lifelike textures and natural color variations. The devil really is in the details here. To
  enhance their “realness,” think about nestling them in potting soil or dressing them
  up with finishing rocks or a layer of natural moss. Place these botanical imposters in
  spots where their living counterparts would flourish, such as by a sunny window or in
  a well-lit nook.

  If you’re aiming for a lush look, make sure to introduce a variety of faux-plants in
  different heights and species to mirror nature’s own diversity. A subtle adjustment
  of leaves and stems can give them a more natural posture, steering clear of the
  unnaturally stiff and perfect appearance that often gives them away. And just like
  living plants, keeping them dust-free and clean will keep them looking fresh and
  cared for.

  I had a client who was renting a beautiful Spanish home in the Fairfax District of Los
  Angeles. Unfortunately, in the middle of her dining room wall, there was a vertical
  window that looked out to the neighbor’s utility box - UGLY!

  The space between the two houses wasn’t easy to access so watering and pruning
  was going to be a problem. She couldn’t plant a tree or build a fence - but what she
  could do was put a faux-hedge in front of the window to make it look like there was
  a boxwood hedge! She placed the faux-screen a few feet away from her window to
  give it depth and suddenly, it looked like she was in a garden - all for under $100. It
  completely transformed the room.

  With these detailed and thoughtful touches, your faux-plants can effortlessly
  enhance your space, bringing in the beauty and fresh energy of the outdoors – no
  watering required.

                         LET’S CLEAR THE AIR




  apartmenttherapy.com



  While many of us love the idea that our houseplants are purifying the air in our
  homes, recent research suggests that this belief might be more of a myth than a
  reality.

  So how did this become such a common belief in the world of plants?

  This green tale sprouted from a NASA study in the 1980s, where researchers
  discovered that in the controlled confines of a lab, certain plants were adept at
  whisking away airborne nasties like formaldehyde. This exciting revelation led many
  to embrace the notion that our leafy companions could be natural air purifiers in the
  cozy corners of our living rooms and bedrooms. Sounds amazing, right?

  Well, subsequent research has gently nudged us back to reality, indicating that
  plants’ air-cleansing powers in the average home environment fall short of those
  lab-controlled wonders. The truth is, for plants to significantly purify indoor air, we’d
  need a veritable jungle in our living spaces (which is far beyond what’s practical for
  most of us, unless you’re a plant influencer!).

         WELCOME TO THE JUNGLE
  Speaking of jungles…we cannot have a conversation about plants without addressing
  the elephant in the room. This phenomenon is what I like to call the over-ambitious
  plant enthusiast.

  If you were scrolling online anytime between March 2020 up until very recently, you
  probably noticed tons of images of people’s homes with wall-to-wall plants adorning
  every nook and cranny of their space. These spaces look like literal jungles, where
  not a surface is spared from the leafy foliage of a mass of green babies.

  And while these verdant interiors can look breathtaking, the reality of living in a plant-
  packed paradise isn’t always as glamorous or practical as it seems.

  There’s a fine line between tasteful greenery and a botanical overload. Overdoing
  it can quickly tip the scales from cozy to chaotic. Imagine trying to keep up with the
  watering, pruning, and care needs of a veritable army of plants, each with its own
  preferences for light, moisture, and temperature. It’s a daunting task that could easily
  overwhelm even the most dedicated of plant lovers – especially if you’re juggling a
  busy schedule, chasing after kids, or simply seeking a low-maintenance lifestyle.

  Beyond that, too many plants in a room can create visual clutter. Just like any other
  element of interior design, plants need to be integrated mindfully into your space.
  An excess of plants might detract from other design elements, preventing your home
  from feeling balanced and cohesive. They can also make your room feel smaller and
  less organized.

  Sometimes, you truly can have too much of a good thing.

  So, what if you’re serious about that “plant life” and want to have a botanical oasis in
  your home?

  As always, you want to keep your big picture perspective in mind. First and foremost,
  think about what it is you want to manifest in your life. Are you going for blissful zen?
  Power and success? Decide what plants will amplify those desires. If you want to
  manifest a soft and sensual life, a bunch of cacti are probably not going to bring in
  the energy that you’re looking for.

  you’ve chosen so far. What plants vibe with your selections? If your style leans heavily
