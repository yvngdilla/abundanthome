 Ok, let’s get started. You know I love to start thinking about everything at the
 driveway/entrance!

 I know, I know, I can hear you now… “But Nancy, how can you start at the driveway

 think about it this way:
 Window treatments affect the outside of your home, too! It’s important to consider
 the horizontal and vertical lines of the treatments from the exterior as well as the
 interior of your home. And don’t forget the backing of the drapes and shades!
 Awareness of these little details is important; that’s when the vibrational energy
 really starts to resonate. Thrill bumps!!

 outfit that boosts your house’s curb appeal. But these beauties aren’t just for show.

 Inside, they’re working hard to keep your home comfortable and energy-efficient.
 Lined curtains and shades act as insulators, keeping your home warm in the chilly
 months and cool during the warmer ones, which can mean big savings on your
 energy bill.

 And in the bedroom? Blackout lining is the stuff of dreams – literally-blocking out
 early morning light for those lazy sleep-ins (and even muffling outside noise). Plus,
 they help to protect fabrics and wood flooring from sun damage and fading, helping
 to extend their life and keep your home looking oh-so-fine.

 If you ask me, it’s a win-win-win!

        QUESTIONS TO ASK YOURSELF BEFORE
               MAKING A DECISION


  Overall Look and Feeling:
  Decide on the desired final look. Pull out your Project Board and review your design style.
  Whether it’s Modernafriscandi, Bohowarmzen, or Frenchcountryjapandicottagecore, choose
  layers that contribute to this style.
  • Do I need versatility for one space with multiple uses (ie. bedroom/office)
  • What type of lighting does my home have?
  • Do I need temperature or light control?
  • Do I need precise control?

  Do I need versatility for one space with multiple uses? (ie. bedroom/office)
  The window treatment needs will vary from room to room. Are there special requirements like
  blackout or maybe reflection on your monitor or TV screen you need to consider?

  Do I need temperature or light control?
  Do you need to consider keeping the heat or light in or with my fabric selection? Do I need
  to be able to open the windows to let the fresh air and light in?

  Do I need precise control?
  Do the circumstances change during the day or night and how much effort do you want to
  put into that change? Would it be easier and more efficient to open drapes or tilt a shutter
  or raise a blind?

  Purpose and Functionality:
  Define the primary purpose of each layer. Sheer curtains can offer light and privacy during
  the day, while heavier drapes can provide insulation and block light at night. Also take into
  consideration if you have determined for your window to be a focal point in your design.

  Color Coordination:
  Ensure the colors of different layers complement or work with each other and the overall
  room decor. Avoid clashing patterns unless you’re aiming for a bold, eclectic look.

                              TOP IT OFF
  If you’re aiming to give your window treatments that extra touch of flair or you want to
  add layers, valances, and cornices may be something you want to consider.

  These decorative accents sit at the top of your windows, elegantly concealing
  hardware and completing the look of blinds, shades, or draperies.

  Here’s the skinny on these whopper toppers:

  Valances are made from fabric that cover the top part of windows. They can be simple
  and straight, scalloped, gathered, pleated, or designed in a swag style, draping from
  rods or boards.

  Cornices, on the other hand, offer a more structured look. They are box-like
  structures made from wood or similar materials that can be covered in fabric, painted,
  or wallpapered. They come in various shapes and can also be adorned with trims
  and moldings. Cornices conceal hardware, add architectural depth to spaces, and
  enhance a window’s prominence with a polished finish. Because of this, they are
  ideal for more formal settings or in a space where you want to add a feeling of stately
  sophistication.

  If this sounds kind of cool to you but you’re worried about your space looking too
  dated, there are lots of options to experiment with to help achieve a contemporary
  look. Try incorporating classic patterns like florals or checks (in updated colors or
  scales) for a timeless look. Or, consider mixing textures to add depth – think velvet
  with sheer fabrics. It’s also fun to work with traditional styles such as Roman shades
  but in simplified designs or modern fabrics instead of the gaudy, ornate styles and
  patterns of yester-year.

  Don’t be afraid to add classic accessories like tassels or fringes – just remember to
  use them strategically, or in fresh new ways.

  Have fun!

               BEHIND THE CURTAIN
   Let’s talk about what I consider to be probably the easiest window treatments for a
   quick and easy upgrade – curtains and drapes.

   They require minimal effort to install and can instantly transform the look and feel of a
   room. With a wide variety of colors, patterns, and fabrics available, they offer endless
   possibilities to match your own personal style.

   Hanging them is as straightforward as choosing a rod and hooks or rings. Plus,
   curtains and drapes not only help to add style and a nice finishing touch, but also
   provide functional benefits like light control, privacy, and insulation. On top of all
   that, you can use curtains and drapes to help you create some design “trickery” to
   change up your space in ways you may have not thought about before!

                       MILE HIGH CLUB
  Raise the roof! I’ll let you in on a little secret – there’s a way to significantly impact the
  visual height of your room’s space – with window treatments! How? By raising your
  curtains vertically.

  Instead of installing your rod just a few inches above the window frame, try raising it
  closer to your ceiling. With this little tip, you can trick the eye and give the illusion of
  a taller space. Use the ⅔ to determine the proper spacing.

  So what if you want to do this, but your window treatments are too short?

  A super simple option is to use tabs or hooks with rings to help extend the length of
  the curtains. Use this method mindfully, as it will only give you a few inches at most.

  If you’re handy, or are willing to pay for an alteration, a helpful tip is to add extra fabric
  to the top or bottom of your existing panel.

  Be sure to use what you know about color and scale so that you get the best results
  here.

  Other ways to add height to your space with curtains:

         • Track Curtains: Installing curtains on ceiling-mounted tracks further
         emphasizes the height of the room. The uninterrupted line from the track
         to the floor can visually elongate the space, offering a sleek and modern
         aesthetic.

         • Integrated Curtains: Seamlessly incorporating curtains into the room’s
         architecture, such as with built-in recesses or moldings, can create an
         illusion of height. This approach unifies the window treatments with the
         room’s structure, promoting a sense of grandeur and spaciousness.

   Curtains and drapes aren’t the only window treatments you can try this method with!
   Installing your shades higher and wider can do the trick, too. This can be particularly
   helpful with small and awkward-sized windows.

    HOW TO MAKE YOUR DRAPES
      LOOK FULL AND LUSH
   Just like you can trick the eye with height, the same goes with width. Here, you want
   to bring your window treatments further past your actual window, width-wise, to give
   an effect of horizontal expansion.

   To ensure an unobstructed view when your curtains are open, it’s a good practice to
   extend your curtain rod by 10-15 inches past each side of the window. This allows the
   curtain panels to rest against the walls instead of covering the window, maximizing
   your view and natural light.

   However, for a fuller and more voluminous curtain look, think about extending your
   rod even further.

   I like to go 20 inches on each side of the window. Or, if you’re aiming for an even more
   luxurious and dramatic effect, extend the rod all the way to the ends of the walls on
   both sides. A full wall of curtains can look very chic and high-end, but can also work
   wonders in rooms with off-center windows, as it helps balance the space and create
   a more symmetrical appearance.

   Along with raising up your treatments, this is probably the easiest and most cost-
   effective way to make a dramatic impact with windows in your space.

   If you decide to extend your curtains horizontally, you’ll want to make sure that they
   stay looking full and substantial, and not thin or sparse when your curtains are closed.
   Make sure to purchase either wider panels or multiple panels to pull off this look (see
   Pro Tips).

   Something to Consider: If you have curtain material on the thinner side (like linen)
   you will want to make sure that you purchase them lined to make sure that you can’t
   see the wall behind them, which will ruin the illusion that you are trying to create.

            A SUPER SIMPLE TWEAK
   Keeping in the spirit of the K.I.S.S. method, you often don’t have to change your actual
   curtains or drapery to make a big impact. Sometimes all you need is a little hardware
   tweak. We touched on this in Key 3 Element 3, where we talked about hardware. Be
   mindful of how impactful your hardware can be here!

   When deciding how to swap out your hardware, think about your personal design
   style and the look and feeling you’re cultivating in your home (Your inspiration and
   the choices that you’ve already made will help to guide your selection!).

   Maybe you just need a finish swap from brass to chrome. Or perhaps “beefing” up
   your rod will do the trick. What about layering your curtains? Is it time to get a double
   rod?

   Check out some types of curtain rods you may want to consider for your space:

   • Standard: Simple, straight rods, suitable for most curtains and homes.
   • Double: Allows for layering of curtains, such as sheers and drapes.
   • Tension: No drilling required, ideal for lightweight curtains and rentals.
   • Concealed: Designed to be hidden from view to provide a clean and uncluttered
   look. They fit neatly behind the curtain or in a recess and allow the focus to stay on
   the fabric of the curtain. Great for those who prefer unadorned and minimal spaces.
   • Traverse: Built-in pulley system for smoothly operating heavy drapes. Ideal for large
   or hard-to-reach windows, traverse rods ensure uniform, neat draping and can be
   customized to various window sizes.
   • Track: Provides a sleek, modern look and allows smooth gliding of curtains. Can
   install on the ceiling for floor-to-ceiling treatments.
   • Return (French Return): Wraps around to the wall for a polished look (and light
   control).
   • Café: Perfect for short window treatments in kitchens or informal spaces. Allows
   light to come in from the top, uncovered portion of the window.

                 HEAD OF THE CLASS
   We can’t talk about curtains and drapes without talking about how they are finished
   at the top. You’ve got a lot of options, but here are a few of the top choices to get you
   percolating:

   • Plain Tops feature a straightforward, unadorned top edge without any pleats or
   folds that can be hung using various methods, such as with curtain rings or directly
   on a rod. This option is suitable for a wide range of fabrics, patterns, and design
   styles.

   • Rod Pockets are a simple, casual style in which the curtain fabric is sewn to create
   a pocket that slides directly onto the rod. This style is ideal for lightweight fabrics and
   offers a gathered look.

   • Pinch Pleats are when the curtain fabric is gathered into groups of three or more
   pleats, pinched at the base to create a tailored look. This option creates a very
   finished and designer-y look.

   • Box Pleats create deep folds down the full length of the curtains. The pleats are
   spaced evenly, creating a neat, boxy look, suitable for a classic or contemporary feel.

   • Pencil Pleats feature tightly gathered folds at the top, resembling the size of pencils.
   This style is quite versatile and works well with various decor styles.

   • Ripplefold: is known for its modern, sleek look, Ripplefold curtains have uniform,
   gentle folds. The fabric is attached to a track with snap tape, creating a smooth,
   continuous wave effect. A nice choice for a contemporary aesthetic and works well
   with sheer to medium-weight fabrics.

   • Ruffles offer a decorative frill or ruffle along the top, adding a romantic, feminine
   touch. It’s a great choice for shabby chic or cottage-style interiors (Gilmore Girls,
   anyone??).

   • Tab Tops feature loops (tabs) at the top of the curtain through which the rod
   passes. This style is easy to hang and suits casual, rustic, or country-style decors.

   • Back/Hidden Tabs are similar to tab tops, but the tabs are hidden on the back of
   the curtain, offering a clean look with soft, loose folds.

   • Grommets or Eyelets are curtains with metal rings (grommets) at the top. They
   slide easily on the rod and suit a modern, minimalist aesthetic. This choice is ideal for
   heavier fabrics.

                         HANGING AROUND
   If you decide on putting up curtains or drapery yourself, you’ll need to think about the style
   and practicality of how they’ll hang. Do you prefer a clean, minimalist look, or something
   more whimsical and romantic? Are there pets or kids who might tug on longer panels? Your
   lifestyle and aesthetic preferences are key factors in deciding how to hang your drapery.
   Here are some options to tailor the installation to both your desired look and your daily life:

  1. Kiss the Floor: Hanging curtains so they just touch the floor creates a clean, tailored
  look. This style is practical, easy to maintain, and suits most decor styles. It’s ideal for high-
  traffic areas where longer curtains might get in the way.

  2. Puddle on the Ground: Allowing extra length so the curtains gently pool on the floor
  offers a luxurious, romantic feel. This works well in formal settings or for a more dramatic,
  opulent look. However, keep in mind it requires more maintenance and isn’t ideal for high-
  traffic areas.

  3. Float Above the Floor: Curtains hanging an inch or so above the floor is practical for
  spaces with pets or children. It gives a neat, uncluttered look, suitable for a more casual,
  laid-back style.

  4. Break at the Floor: A slight break or bend at the floor, where the curtain extends just a
  bit longer than the floor, strikes a balance between a clean look and a touch of luxury.

  5. Sweeping the Floor: Similar to the ‘break at the floor’ but with a bit more length, these
  curtains gracefully sweep the floor, providing an elegant look that’s slightly more dramatic
  than just kissing or breaking at the floor.

  Just like curtains, shades offer versatility, functionality, and style. They come in numerous
  styles, colors, and materials, easily fitting into just about any decor.

  Shades offer a tremendous amount of control over both light and privacy, with options
  ranging from light-filtering sheers to room-darkening blackout options. You can tailor them

  Ready to spruce up your windows with some stylish shades? Picking the right mount can
  make all the difference! Let’s explore your options:

  Inside Mount: This option involves fitting the shades within the window frame. It offers a

  unattractive window frames. Outside mounts can provide better light control and privacy
  since they cover the entire window. They are also more forgiving in terms of measurement
  and installation. This type of mounting is a good choice for windows with shallow depth or
  for covering an area wider than the window itself.

                     SHUTTER ISLAND
  Shutters can offer a classic and stylish look to your home, but like any design choice,
  they come with their own set of pros and cons. On the upside, shutters provide
  excellent light control and privacy. Their ability to adjust the amount of light entering
  a room is a significant advantage, and when closed, they offer complete privacy.
  Aesthetically, shutters can add a timeless elegance to a space, enhancing the overall
  design with their structured look. They also come in a variety of materials and colors,
  allowing for customization to suit different interior styles.

  However, shutters do have some drawbacks. Shutters can be more expensive than
  other window treatments, both in terms of the product and installation. They also
  require a fair amount of space around the window for proper operation, which might
  not work in more compact areas and could be challenging with furniture placement.

  Something else to consider is that shutters can block your view, especially if they
  are fixed directly onto the windows. This might not be ideal for you if you enjoy
  unobstructed outdoor views or for rooms where natural scenery is a focal point.

  Some people don’t care for shutters because, well…they never go away. Even if you
  open the shutter panels up, they have to rest at the sides of your windows. So if
  you’re someone who prefers that your window treatments “disappear” like shades or
  blinds can, maybe skip shutters for your home.

  WINDOW TREATMENT PRO TIPS

  • Hang curtains at the perfect height with drapery hooks. Instead of trying to install
  your curtain rod at the exact perfect height, using hooks with curtain rings lets you
  easily adjust your curtains to just the right height.

  • Mount your curtain rod 10-15” above the window or 2-6” down from the ceiling.

  • Buy French return curtain rods to help with light coming through at the sides of
  your windows.

  • If you want full looking curtains when panels are closed, make sure the width of
  your panels match the width of the rod. For example, if the length of your rod extends
  15” on either side and your window is 60” wide, each window panel should be around
  90” wide (Keep in mind, this is dependent on the thickness of the fabric, header type,
  and hardware).

  • Opt for cordless window shades for child and pet safety.

  • Get samples of your window treatments and check them out in your space. Take
  a look at them throughout the day in natural and artificial light to make sure they
  work in your space.

  • Install shades with an outside mount to mask awkward, worn, or small windows.
