  MAKING IT REAL WITH INSPIRED ACTION
   You received a lot of information throughout THE GUIDE about how to use color, texture,
   aroma, music, and even food to manifest your dream life. Still, you need to know how to pull
   it all together. You need a solid plan.

   The Universe will provide your manifestations, but it’s not gonna move the sofa or create
   the project schedule, right?

   The vibrational alignment of your thoughts is the foundation for manifesting, but my AHD
   methods have taken this to the next level by making changes to your environment to
   physically support your vibrational alignment message.

   And that requires action…inspired action! Action that is guided by a unifying vision that
   details the specifics of your Newlife.

   Even when we’re aiming for low-hassle changes, there are still a few key things you’ll want
   to have on your radar to make sure everything runs smoothly as you implement your design
   plan.

   So with that in mind, here are a few things that you’ll want to keep in mind as you work
   through your project:

   • Design Vision: Circle back to your Project and Vision Board. Are you still vibing with
       your initial picks? As I’ve mentioned throughout this course, we grow and change –
       and so does our design vision for our home. So it’s totally fine if you want or need to
       tweak things. You’ve learned so much, it will be easy for you to go back and refine your
       selections.
   •   Budget: Nail down your spending plan. Know your limits for each area you plan to take
       on – paint, furniture, decorations – to keep your finances happy.
   •   Color Scheme: Use your chosen palette to pin down the actual paint colors you’re
       going to splash on those walls.
   •   Samples, Research, and Shopping: Test that paint where you’ll actually use it. Hunt
       for furniture and decor that fit your vision, style, budget, and quality standards. This
       might mean surfing the web, hitting stores, or picking up samples.
   •   Scheduling: List or sketch out what you need to do to complete your project. You can
       also list out when each task will happen, especially if you’re coordinating with professional
       services or getting help from friends or family. Aim for a schedule that keeps your life
       smooth and disruption-free.
   •   Preparation: Set the stage for painting or new arrivals. Shift or cover furniture, fix up
       walls, or clear space for incoming pieces.

                                           BUDGET
   The truth is that EVERYONE has a budget. Creating a budget may seem boring or tedious, especially
   to those of us who are mathematically challenged. But I promise you – budgets are your friend!

   Like defining your style, setting your intentions, and getting super clear with the Universe, a budget
   can help to give you a bit of structure and guidance. It streamlines the decision-making process,
   making it easier to achieve your desired outcomes with ease and simplicity.

   Breaking your budget up by category can really help, too. Figure out how much you are willing to
   spend on paint, furniture, fabrics, and decor items. This will guide your choices and help prevent
   pesky overspending (and might even help you save for future projects!).

   When you are creating your budget, take a little time to do some research. If you’re willing to spend
   a little more on certain items, make sure that it’s worth it. Is the more expensive option truly the
   best option or are you just paying for a label…or a celebrity endorsement? Look for options that fit
   within your budget while still meeting your design standards and manifestation goals. Good design
   does not have to cost a lot of money.

          ORDER OF OPERATIONS – MAKE A
             DECORATING SCHEDULE
   Not a fan of making lists? I get it, but jotting things down can be a game-changer for keeping your
   project smooth and your thoughts organized.

   Start by listing out what you need to do for your project – things like ordering furniture, choosing
   paint, picking out fabrics, finding the right decor, or even hiring tradesmen to do tasks like painting
   and installing fixtures. Don’t stress, though. Even decluttering needs a list…boxes, labels, markers,
   and bags, making space in the car…etc.

   This list isn’t a race; it’s more like a roadmap to get you where you want to go.

   I find it super helpful to prioritize my project tasks and lead times. For instance, I tackle the big stuff
   first, like furniture shopping. These pieces usually take the longest to arrive, especially if you’re
   customizing them. And since you don’t buy things like sofas or beds as often as, say, throw pillows,
   it makes sense to secure these key pieces early and then let the rest of your design flow from there.

   Need to bring in a pro like a plumber, electrician, or handyman? Don’t leave it till the last second.
   These folks can get booked up fast (and let’s face it, they can sometimes be a bit unreliable),
   so it’s wise to lock someone down early to make sure your project doesn’t hit a snag. Getting
   recommendations from friends or family you trust can also go a long way in ensuring you hire
   someone who’ll get the job done right.



         AND…ACTION – INSPIRED ACTION!
   Alright, you’ve got your to-do list ready, so it’s time to nail down those final design choices and
   jump into action! Exciting, right?

   First things first, gather all your bits and pieces: your Project and Vision Boards, floor and furniture
   layouts, samples of paint and fabric, a list of items you’re keeping and what you need to buy, decor
   pieces on your wishlist, and your budget. It’s time to piece together your final design scheme with
   these selections.

   Lay it all out and take a good look at your project as a whole with that big picture perspective!

   Are there any last-minute tweaks needed? Does that tan sofa complement the paint color you’ve
   chosen? Check if the size and scale of your items fit well in the space, and how the finishes (and
   things like the furniture legs) look together. Do they complement each other?
