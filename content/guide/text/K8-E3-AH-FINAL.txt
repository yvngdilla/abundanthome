                              TASTEMAKER
   Do you love to devour a big slice of decadent chocolate cake? Or are crispy, salty
   chips more your bag?

   Well, it’s all a matter of taste.

   Taste is one of our most fascinating and personal senses. What tastes amazing to
   one person can feel like an assault on the senses to another.

   Like music and aroma, we can use food to ignite our senses and explore and interact
   with the world around us. From the first bite of a new dish to the comfort of a familiar
   flavor, taste allows us to make immediate and visceral judgments about what we do
   like, and what we don’t.

   Taste is the sensation we experience when we eat or drink something. It’s how we
   detect, describe, and distinguish flavors. When food touches our tongue, it interacts
   with tiny sensors that send messages to our brain about what we’re tasting – we call
   them taste buds. It’s like we have a little communication system between our mouth
   and brain, telling us if something is delicious, yucky, or somewhere in between.

   However, our sense of taste does not work alone. It is deeply intertwined with other
   senses – especially smell, which can significantly enhance the flavors we perceive. If
   you’ve ever had a stuffy nose, you know how irritating it can be to try to eat – things
   just taste “off.” The National Library of Medicine writes that taste (along with smell),
   is closely linked to our emotions because it is connected to the involuntary nervous
   system. That’s why you can get physically sick from a bad taste or smell.

   On top of that, flavors that we love increase the production of saliva and gastric
   juices. They don’t call it mouthwatering for nothing! Our bodies are so fascinating,
   aren’t they?

   This multisensory experience helps us recognize a wide variety of tastes. From
   the basic sweet, salty, sour, and bitter to the more nuanced and complexness of
   umami. It’s how you can appreciate the subtleties in a fine wine, the rich bitterness
   of chocolate, or the sweetness of a ripe piece of fruit.

   Like with most things in life, our taste and preferences can evolve over time, mirroring
   the twists and turns of our own lives. Experiences, exposure to different cultures,
   people, influence, and even emotional states can trigger new wants and desires.
   Taste allows us to explore the world in a highly personal way which can lead to a
   direct path of pleasure, discovery, and sometimes even adventure! And what we
   once disliked may become a favored indulgence. I mean, I don’t think most of us
   come out of the womb loving the slurp of raw oysters or an uber-hoppy IPA!

   Taste plays a huge role in what we crave and truly love to eat, and what we’d rather
   skip. Let’s talk about the 5 basic categories of taste your taste buds can identify:

   1. Sweet: This is the taste of sugar and foods that contain sugar. It’s usually a pleasant
      taste that we often associate with desserts, fruits, and anything sugary.
   2. Salty: This taste comes from salt. We can find it in foods like chips, nuts, and any
      food that has been seasoned with salt. It’s used to enhance the flavor of food
      (even in sweet treats!). If you’ve ever forgotten to add salt to a recipe, you know
      how important it is in cooking and baking.
   3. Sour: Sour taste is like the tanginess of a lemon or lime. It can be found in sour
      candies, some dairy products like yogurt, and fruits like green apples and oranges.
   4. Bitter: Bitterness is a more complex, often considered less pleasant and can
      be an acquired taste. It’s found in foods like dark chocolate, coffee, and certain
      vegetables like kale.
   5. Savory/Umami: Umami is a savory taste, often described as meaty or brothy. It’s
      found in foods like tomatoes, mushrooms, nutritional yeast, soy sauce, miso, and
      meats. Umami adds depth and a rounded fullness to the flavor of food.

                         MINDFUL EATING




     marcusnilsson.com


   Just like energetic eating, mindful eating invites you to forge a stronger relationship
   with what’s on your plate.

   With energetic eating, the focus is on the food itself. But with mindful eating, it’s all
   about the experience of eating. Eating mindfully is all about immersing yourself in
   the moment, taking the time to truly appreciate each bite. Mindful eating turns your
   meals into a full on sensory experience, encouraging you to notice its colors, textures,
   flavors, and aromas, while also tuning into your body’s signals of hunger and satiety.

   Ever found yourself mindlessly munching through a bag of popcorn while watching
   TV, only to realize it’s halfway gone before the show’s even begun? That’s exactly the
   kind of autopilot behavior that mindful eating aims to transform, making every meal a
   moment of deep engagement and gratitude.

   In a world where diet culture is loud and everywhere, telling us to see food as either
   a reward or a punishment, mindful eating offers a refreshing pause. It helps us move
   past the idea that we “deserve” a treat for behaving a certain way. This mindset can
   lead to a cycle where we either deny ourselves the joy of food or eat beyond our
   needs.

   Mindful eating encourages us to step away from the guilt, anxiety, and narratives we
   often weave around our eating habits and body image. Instead, it guides us to a place
   where we can enjoy our meals for what they are — nourishing, enjoyable, and a part of
   our daily pleasures.

    Ways to eat mindfully:

   • Eat without distraction. That means turn off the TV and put away your phone.
   • Notice the physical and sensory experiences associated with eating. How does it
       feel, what does it taste like, what does it smell like?
   •   Acknowledge your response to the food you are consuming (likes, dislikes, or
       neutral) without judgment.
   •   Be aware of hunger and fullness cues to guide decisions to begin and end eating.

      THERE’S SOMETHING ABOUT NANCY




  The Holiday (2006)


 Ah, Nancy Meyers movies – where the kitchens and homes are as much a character as the
 leading ladies themselves! I know this Element is all about food, but let’s wrap it up and talk a
 little about what inspires those delicious meals.

 If you’re not familiar with Nancy Meyers, all you need to know is that the sets of her movies
 have a bit of a cult following – and people love to talk about them!

 Funny enough, a lot of people watch her films and find themselves just as invested in the
 stunning interiors as they are in the protagonist’s journey to find love. Their style might not
 necessarily be your jam, but there’s something undeniably aspirational about them.

 In her movies, you’ll always find a beautifully designed kitchen where characters whip up
 midnight chocolate croissants or sit around a large island, and enjoy a glass of wine and a
 plate of scrambled eggs with Jack Nicholson. The kitchens often feel cozy, effortless, and a
 place where you’d want to gather.

 So, if you love cooking and food is a big part of who you are, then you should think about
 creating your own Nancy Meyers kitchen. Now, I’m not talking about a duplication of one of
 her set designs or a particular style. I’m talking about creating a kitchen that inspires you and
 feels like a slice of the life you want to live!

 Here are some fun and easy ways to make your kitchen feel more special, inviting, and primed
 for culinary creativity:

 • Light it Right: Think soft, warm lights that illuminate your space. Under-cabinet lighting,
     small table lamps, and candles can all add both function and ambiance, making chopping
     and prep work easier while adding a cozy glow.
 •   Open Shelving and Glass-Front Cabinets: If you’ve got them, they are a great way to
     display your prettiest dishes, glassware, and even your spices or cookbooks. It’s a way to
     add a personal touch and keep your most-loved items within easy reach.
 •   An Island: If space allows, consider adding an island. It doesn’t have to be big or
     expensive. You can find some pretty nice moveable ones on Facebook Marketplace or
     Ikea. Your new island can be a prep area, a gathering spot for friends and family, or provide
     additional storage or display space for some kitchen accessories.
 •   Quality Cookware on Display: If you have pots that you love – show them off! Hang
     your copper pots and pans or store them on a pot rack. Leave your beautiful stand mixer
     out on the counter in all its glory. Not only are they functional, but they also add a touch
     of professional kitchen chic.
 •   Herbs and Plants: Fresh herbs in pots not only bring greenery into your kitchen but are
     also handy for cooking. Plus, they’re a simple way to add life and a splash of color.
 •   Personal Touches: Whether it’s a family heirloom on the countertop or your collection
     of vintage kitchen timers, adding personal touches makes the space uniquely yours.
 •   Create a Tea, Coffee or Cocktail Corner: Dedicate a small section of your countertop
     to a tea or coffee station or a cocktail mixing area. It’s a functional addition that feels
     special, luxurious, and inviting.
 •   Comfortable Seating: If space permits, add a small nook with comfortable seating and
     throw pillows for a cozy cocooned feeling. If a nook doesn’t work, a small table with chairs
     is perfect for sipping your morning coffee or for guests to chat with you as you cook a
     delicious meal.

 Adding those little touches to your kitchen, regardless of its size or state, can turn meal prep
 from a chore into a delight. It’s all about the vibe – when your kitchen feels good, you feel
 good.

 When you’re in a high vibe state, aligned with the energy of your inner being, this positivity
 will flow into your cooking. And when you eat your food, it will nourish not just your body but
 fill your heart with love and a sense of abundance. That’s the magic of creating a kitchen with
 intention!

 So go ahead, let your kitchen be a source of joy and inspiration, and watch as love and
 abundance fills your meals, your belly, and your heart.
