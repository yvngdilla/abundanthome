                                             PAST

The connection between aroma and memory
is a deep and fascinating one. Our sense of
smell is directly connected to our brain’s limbic
system, which is responsible for emotions
and memories. That’s why catching a whiff
of a long-forgotten smell can evoke deep
emotions, or uncover a whisper of a distant
memory that you thought was lost forever to
the sands of time.

When we encounter a particular scent, it can
unlock memories that may have been long
forgotten. This phenomenon is often referred
to as the “Proustian phenomenon,” named
after the French writer Marcel Proust, who
famously described the involuntary memory
recall triggered by the scent of a madeleine
cookie in his novel “In Search of Lost Time.”

Scent holds a deep and powerful connection to nostalgia - and you can use this to your
advantage while bringing your Newlife Vision into reality.

My friend Paige has always felt a deep connection to the smell of freshly cut wood. It reminds
her of when she was a little girl when she would visit her grandparent’s house. Whenever she
went into the garage, her Grandfather was always doing woodworking, and it always smelled
of freshly cut wood. Even decades later, the memory is still clear for her. It evokes feelings of
nostalgia, safety, warmth, comfort, and the love of her grandparents.

When Paige started her manifestation journey, she realized that incorporating fresh smells of
wood into her home would help her create that same atmosphere for herself, even as a grown
woman. She loves to use Sandalwood, Cedar, Palo Santo, and other natural scents of trees and
the woodlands to evoke those same feelings. As a result, her home is always a sanctuary of
safety, warmth, and comfort. She even takes a stick of Palo Santo with her when she travels so
that she can bring that atmosphere with her no matter where she goes.

   However, if you’d like to think (or smell!) outside the box here, consider some other
   aromatic options!

   One of my favorite ways to cultivate the sensory experience in my home is with
   burning scented oils or resins.

   These resins are natural compounds secreted by certain trees and plants. The most
   well-known types include frankincense, myrrh, copal, and benzoin. When heated,
   they emit a fragrant smoke that is used for a variety of purposes, including spiritual
   rituals, meditation, purification of spaces, and transforming the energy in your home.

   But, as nice as this smoke can smell, it might not be great for everyone. If you have
   asthma, allergies, or trouble breathing, you might want to think twice. Also, the smoke
   from burning resins can make the air quality in your house worse and leave a sticky
   residue on things that can be a pain to clean off.

   Good news, though! To get around all the smoke, all you need is an aromatherapy oil
   warmer (available in ceramic or glass designs) to burn your incense. These warmers
   are easy to find in stores that sell candles and similar accessories. Although ceramic
   warmers are usually marketed for wax cubes, they work just fine for resin incense
   too.




ABUNDANT HOME
                                           AROMA




  Here’s how you do it:



  1. Add Oil to the Warmer Dish: Pour 1 to 2 teaspoons of a light oil (like vegetable
     or sunflower oils) into the dish, filling it just under halfway. Avoid thick oils (such
     as coconut, jojoba, or argan). Skip essential oils to prevent altering the scent of
     the incense. Don’t use water either, as it will evaporate. Oil will prevent the dish
     from getting too hot and cracking.
  2. Incorporate Resin Incense: Add around 1 teaspoon of powdered resin incense
     or a few pea-sized chunks if it’s solid. Choose from a variety of resins, depending
     on your preference.
  3. Light a Tea Light Candle: Place an unscented tea light in the oil warmer before
     or after lighting it. Use only a tea light to ensure it fits and burns correctly under
     the dish.
  4. Heat the Oil: Allow a few minutes for the oil to heat up and the resin to warm and
     release its aroma. The time it takes may vary based on the oil warmer’s material
     and the amount of oil used.
  5. Extinguish the Candle When Done: You can snuff out the candle after use or
     let it burn out naturally. Most tea lights last about 4 hours, but longer-burning
     options are also available.
  6. Safety First: Never leave the oil warmer unattended while the candle is lit to
     ensure a safe and enjoyable incense experience!

  Simmer pots are an amazing method for infusing your home with aromatic scents. This
  simple practice involves simmering a combination of fruits, herbs, and spices in water
  on the stove, creating a fragrant atmosphere that permeates throughout your space.

  These simmering pots are especially popular during the holidays due to their common
  ingredients being seasonally-focused, and they are practical for winter since having
  the stove on for an extended period-of-time creates heat and warmth in the kitchen.
  However, they can be used all year round, and can easily be catered to your specific
  tastes, preferences, and desires.

  Give this a try if you’ve got a day off that you plan to spend at home. Just bring the
  ingredients to a boil, then turn it down to a low simmer and let it go for hours! Make
  sure to keep an eye on the water level and fill it as necessary so it never gets too low.

    COME ON BABY, LIGHT MY FIRE!




   homestolove.com.au



   Using fire to ignite our senses (literally!) is a tried-and-true method for bringing the
   power of aroma to life in your space. Whether you’re lighting a scented candle, igniting
   an incense stick, burning some Sage or Palo Santo, or firing up a tea-light to heat
   some scented oils, the element of fire is your friend here!

   This is just a friendly little reminder to practice caution when using fire and open
   flame in the home. I have a friend who uses small coals to burn resins (like Copal or
   Frankincense) in her space - and it took some trial and error for her to find the right
   tools to support this habit. If you’re using charcoal for this purpose - make sure you
   have the proper implements! A simple plate or an abalone shell might not be able to
   withstand the level of heat emitted from a burning charcoal. Those coals get really
   hot! I recommend using two layers of material - like an abalone shell on a clay plate,
   or two little decorative clay plates on top of each other. Often the bottom portion of a
   red clay plant pot works perfectly.

                                          AROMA




  Similarly, if you’re leaving a candle to burn freely or to heat some scented oil, just be
  mindful and keep an eye on it. I’m sure this goes without saying - but if you’re not
  used to having open flames going in your home, it’s always good to be reminded to
  proceed with caution and care.

  Have kids, pets, or both? Not to worry - you’ve got options, too. Candle warmers are
  a newer phenomenon that allow for all the benefits of a melted candle - without the
  open flame. If you’ve got a busy household with four-legged friends and/or little ones
  – candle warmers might be just the thing for you. While open flames are romantic and
  moody, they can increase the risk of danger or unexpected fires when you’ve got lots
  of energy, action, and movement in the house.

  Candle warmers come in various styles and models. Some come with their own
  scented “melted wax” cubes, while others come with an overhead lamp to position
  over a traditional candle.

  Candle warmers are both safe and economical. The technology of the warmer melts
  the candle more slowly, meaning you’ll be purchasing candles less frequently than
  you normally would. Wax cubes are great if a strong smell is what you’re after - they
  provide more potency than a traditional scented candle. But, just as with regular
  candles, be mindful of the ingredients if you’re going to opt for a wax cube.
