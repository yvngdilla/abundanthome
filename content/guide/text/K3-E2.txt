                    Okay, are you ready?
                       Here we go!

   Turn up the tunes and get your jam on, ‘cause we’re gonna shake things up a bit!
   You can plan to make a day of it or use one of my favorite methods and break it
   down into 15 minute interval purges. I like to set a timer, and let it rip for 15 minutes.
   Then I reset if I feel I want to do more. I don’t want to overwhelm myself in any way
   about letting things go! You decide how much time you want to devote to this -
   keep it easy and fun!

   Pull out your Heart-Centered Checklist (If you haven’t done that yet, pause here
   and go back to Key 3 Element 1 to complete that exercise). I want you to start at
   the driveway (or your garage or carport - wherever the entrance is to your home).
   Focus on clearing the clutter from your line of sight. Your line of sight is the visual
   and physical energetic pathway that flows through your personal space. At your
   entrance, things that could be blocking your energy could be a pile of broken
   pots, some stuff you’ve “hidden” on your porch, or an old torn-up welcome mat.

   Line of sight and visual clutter also translates to larger items and spaces in your
   home, like your furniture, walls and surfaces. Is there a bookcase at the top of your
   stairs blocking the free passageway? Are your walls and surfaces cluttered with
   too much stuff? I want you to put your attention here first. You can worry about
   your junk drawer and linen closet later - unless you’re really fixated on your bath-
   room drawer and can’t move forward until it is done! If that’s you…go ahead and do
   that first. You can backtrack to the home entrance later, but be sure you do! Your
   entrance is where it all begins.

   Whether you start at your driveway and do a full sweep of your house, or you just
   choose to focus on your bathroom drawer, make sure you do it with the intention
   of manifesting your Newlife Vision.

   Walk through your home just like you did while creating your checklist, but this time
   - you’re going to be taking inspired action. Use the categories below to help you
   sort through your stuff in meaningful ways. Don’t forget to phone a friend if that will
   make this more fun, or if you need a little support! Who knows, they might bring

                     TRASH / RECYCLE

   Because we want this to be easy, we are going to start with TRASH. I’ve found
   that it’s typically the fastest and easiest to do. Immediately throw out any dead (or
   almost dead) plants. Don’t waste another minute trying to revive that one scraggly
   leaf. Let it GO! Get a new one! If there’s something broken, either fix it, or toss it -
   immediately. Stained clothing…OUT IT GOES! Don’t overthink things here. Get out
   of your mind, and into a flow. Have fun, and don’t take this too seriously. Throw it
   out, if it feels right. If not, put it in the “maybe” category. Simple!

   Throughout this process, be sure to recycle everything that you can - magazines,
   newspapers, plastics, etc. Be sure and check your local recycling center’s poli-
   cies and operating procedures. Keep in mind that many recycling centers do not
   accept certain items and have limited hours of operation.

   Think a little outside the box here. Art schools, interior design classes, and elemen-
   tary schools are all great places to donate what you might consider trash. Anything
   that can be donated goes in the “Donate” category (see below).

                                 RETURN
   C’mon…JUST DO IT! Grab anything that needs to be returned and put them in
   your car. Keep it visible, so you don’t forget …and get it done. If you’ve missed the
   return deadline - don’t worry - put it in the “sell” or “donate” category. We’ve all
   been there.




                                   MAYBE
   This “maybe” category is for things you’re undecided about. These items might
   have some value or use, but you’re uncertain about their place in your life or if you
   truly want or need them. For instance, there might be something that you really
   love, but you haven’t worn in years. Or, you might have sentimental attachments,
   or feel guilty about getting rid of something, like that sweater grandma gave you
   back in the day.

   The “maybe” category is meant to give you some time and space to reflect before
   you make a final decision. Instead of getting stuck on “should I or shouldn’t I,” you
   can simply place these items in this bag/box/pile and keep things moving.

   Give yourself a specific time frame for circling back to the items. Once the time is
   up, revisit and see if you’ve gained any clarity about any of them.

   Ask yourself questions like:
         Have I needed this since I’ve put it in the “maybe” category?
         If not, when was the last time that I used it?
         Does this item have a specific and practical use in my life?
         How would my life be affected if I decided to let go of this item?

   PRO TIP:

   I like to keep two bags in my closet on-hand at all times as part of my ongoing
   purging process. I have both a “donate” bag, and a “maybe” bag easily within reach.
   I find this so helpful; even if I’m not in the middle of an active purge or decluttering
   session, I can easily sort items in real time to stay organized, while simultaneously
   making things easier on my future self when I’m doing the actual organizing.

   Give this method a try and see how it feels for you. If you haven’t touched your
   “maybe” items in a while, give yourself a timeframe of 30 days or so. If you haven’t
   used it within those 30 days, put it in the “donate” category.

   Finally, if there are items that you really can’t stand to part with (or that you are so
   on-the-fence about that you feel paralyzed), go ahead and put those items in a
   labeled container that can be kept somewhere out of sight. In 6 months, ask your-
   self, “Am I ready to let these go now?” If you’re not, you probably will be at some
   point in the future. Be kind and gentle with yourself and acknowledge your feelings
   without judgment.

   If you really like something, but it seems a bit off in its current location, maybe it just

                                   DONATE
   Donating items that no longer serve you is a wonderful way to give them a new life,
   and to clear out your space. Maybe you have a stack of sweaters, shirts, or pants
   that have been sitting in your closet, untouched and collecting dust for decades.
   Go through all that “goal weight” clothing that you’ve been hanging onto for months
   (or years). Yes, that groovy shirt that you never wear might come back into style
   one day, but ask yourself, “Am I really ever going to wear this again?”

   Unless, of course, it has costume potential. I keep my costumes in my guest bed-
   room closets!

   When you are donating, Look for causes or organizations that align with your values
   and that have a genuine need for the items you wish to donate. This will likely make
   you feel a lot better about letting go and will make the process more meaningful.
   Check with Art Schools, libraries, and vintage record shops before you toss things
   out. Art Schools love used supplies - almost anything can be repurposed and
   turned into art. There are also plenty of libraries that would appreciate any used
   books or DVD’s.
   It is always good practice to call and check the policies of whatever organization
   you are donating to. You might ask them about drop-off times, acceptable dona-
   tion items, and pick-up services - if they offer any.
   Lastly, make sure that the items are in good condition, clean, and functional. Bro-
   ken or heavily damaged items are typically not suitable for donation and are often
   thrown out. Strive to be a responsible, mindful, and considerate donor.



                                       SELL
   If you can sell it, SELL IT! There are plenty of opportunities to make money off of
   your old things. One man’s ceiling is another man’s floor.
   Selling your vintage clothing to a thrift store or consignment shop is an easy way to
   make a quick buck. I sold my husband’s suits and ties to a Hollywood prop compa-

   If you decide to sell online, make sure to follow these next steps to set yourself up
   for success:


          1. Take Quality Images
          Clear and attractive photos are your best bet to selling your items quickly.
          Natural lighting and neutral backgrounds tend to work best.

          2. Set a Fair Price
          Research the market value of the item so that you know what they are worth
          and can set a fair price that people will be willing to pay you. Be open to
          negotiating, but always know your bottom line so you don’t feel cheated in
          the end.

          3. Be Descriptive
          Write an engaging and accurate description of your listing. Make sure to
          highlight key features, give dimensions, and the condition, along with any
          notable information that buyers should be aware of. Be open and honest
          about any flaws, wear, or tear.

          4. Promote Your Listings
          Share your listings with friends, on social media or relevant online commu-
          nities to increase visibility. The more exposure your items get, the higher the
          chances of a successful sale.

          5. Communicate and Set Expectations
            Be prompt and courteous in your communication with potential buyers.
          Nobody likes to get ghosted, so respond to inquiries in a timely manner.
          Keep in mind that some items may sell quickly, while others may require more
          time to find the right buyer. Manage your buyer’s expectations and remain
          flexible throughout the process to make it the best experience possible.

          6. Scammers
          Be very, very mindful of scammers!! One trick I’ve used is to ask the buyer
          to name their favorite restaurant in the area. If it’s a scam, they will proba-
          bly hang-up. Consider changing your passwords after a transaction. Check
          your bank accounts and credit cards regularly. I don’t want you to sell from
          a place of fear, but being knowledgeable and alert will give you the proper
          tools to sell safely and effectively online or in person.

                                       KEEP
   This category should be the simplest part of this process. The “Keep” category is
   for items that we love, that hold value, or are essential in our lives. You’ll be learn-
   ing a lot more about how to store the stuff you decided to keep, but there is some
   overlap here that relates to decluttering as well.

   If you really love something and you know that you want to keep it - keep it! I’m
   not about guilting people into a minimalist lifestyle. In fact, you’d know that if you
   saw my collection of fun costumes from over the years. FYI - I keep my costumes
   in my guest room closet. Our belongings can and do hold meaning, even if that
   meaning is pure nostalgia. But - If your intention is to create more space for energy
   to flow freely through your home, you might simply need to rethink the way you
   store these keepsakes. For example, you might have a favorite Christmas sweater
   that you wear once a year. I would never tell you to trash that, but I might suggest
   it be folded on the bottom of your stack in the closet, so that you’re not constantly
   having to work around it as you put together outfits for the other 364 days of the
   year (we’ll talk more about Mindful Storage in the next Element).

   While I celebrate your repurposing, displaying, or storing of important items that
   you love, I must suggest that you beware of memorabilia that carries negative emo-
   tions. Whether it be photos of exes, gifts you feel guilty about hating, or diaries
   from really low points in your life - out they go! I promise, getting rid of these items
   will make you feel so much better.
