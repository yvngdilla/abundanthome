           STYLES OF ARTWORK AND
             THEIR ASSOCIATIONS
   Here are some common styles of artwork, along with the emotions and feelings they
   often evoke, connected to specific desires:

   Abstract Art:
   Emotions/Feelings: Freedom, creativity, open-mindedness.
   Desires: Abstract art can be versatile, representing various desires. It may evoke
   feelings of freedom, creativity, and abundance, making it suitable for manifesting
   broad aspirations.

   Impressionism:
   Emotions/Feelings: Tranquility, nostalgia, beauty.
   Desires: Impressionist art often conveys a sense of calm and beauty. It can be aligned
   with desires for peace, harmony, and a tranquil life.

   Realism:
   Emotions/Feelings: Clarity, authenticity, familiarity.
   Desires: Realistic art may be connected to desires for authenticity and clarity, reflecting
   a straightforward approach to life and goals.

   Pop Art:
   Emotions/Feelings: Playfulness, boldness, modernity.
   Desires: Pop art’s vibrant and bold nature can be associated with desires for modernity,
   success in contemporary endeavors, and a playful approach to life.

   Surrealism:
   Emotions/Feelings: Wonder, mystery, introspection.
   Desires: Surrealistic art can be linked to desires for exploration, introspection, and the
   manifestation of unique and unexpected opportunities.

   Expressionism:
   Emotions/Feelings: Intensity, passion, emotion.
   Desires: Expressionist art is often emotionally charged. It may be connected to desires
   for passionate love, intense experiences, or the manifestation of deeply felt goals.

                     PICTURE PERFECT
  I want to take a minute to talk about the different types of glass you can choose.

  Most art aficionados turn their noses up at non-glare products, for good reason. It
  does tend to distort the image when viewing from the side and it can diffuse the
  clarity of some pieces, but to me, so does the reflection from standard glass or acrylic.

  It’s important to recognize how much reflection will occur in the location where you’re
  thinking about installing the art and make your decision based on those conditions.

  Also, if you have a large piece, you might want to consider acrylic. The larger the
  piece of glass, the more vulnerable it can be. Think about door swings and activity
  levels of the space. If your kids and dogs are prone to racing around the corner
  and barreling down the hall right towards the artwork, you might want to use acrylic
  instead of glass for practicality’s sake.




ABUNDANT HOME
                                         ARTWORK




  Here’s a list of different kinds of glass commonly used in framing, along with their
  features and benefits:

  Regular Glass:
  • Material: Standard soda-lime glass
  • Features: Affordable and widely used
  • Considerations: Offers minimal UV protection, can produce glare, and may not be
  ideal for valuable or light-sensitive artwork

  Non-Glare Glass:
  • Material: Treated to reduce reflections
  • Features: Minimizes glare and reflections, improving visibility
  • Considerations: Slight loss of clarity and detail, may require more cleaning than
  regular glass

  Museum Glass:
  • Material: High-quality glass with anti-reflective coatings
  • Features: Provides optimal clarity, reduces reflections, and blocks UV rays
  • Considerations: More expensive than other options but ideal for displaying valuable
  or high-quality artwork

  Acrylic (Plexiglass):
  • Material: Transparent thermoplastic
  • Features: Lightweight, shatter-resistant, and offers UV protection
  • Considerations: More prone to scratches than glass, but popular for large and
  valuable pieces due to its weight and safety benefits


  If you’ve got a lot of precious and expensive pieces to showcase and you want to
  invest in some high-quality coverage to protect them, consider looking into some
  other options like UV Filtering Glass, Optically Clear Acrylic, Anti-Reflective Acrylic,
  or Museum Acrylic.

                                 MIX IT UP




  Who says art in your home has to match? For a fun and exciting space, ditch the
  matchy-matchy approach and embrace the diversity of art!

  Mixing up your artwork isn’t just okay; it’s a brilliant way to inject depth, intrigue, and
  a slice of your personality into your living space. Think of your home as your personal
  gallery, a space where diverse art forms come together to tell a story – your story.

  Museums and galleries often mix different art styles to captivate and surprise their
  audience. You, too, can channel this eclectic, gallery-esque vibe – right in the comfort
  of your home.

  The secret? It’s all about balance.

  Juxtapose different art pieces in a way that they complement and converse with
  each other, not compete for attention. As long as the artwork resonates with your
  style and creates a sense of harmony in your space, you’ve nailed it.

  Ready to mix it up? Here are some simple ways to get the curated look for your
  collection:

  • Unified Color Scheme: Even if the art pieces differ in style and medium, having a
  common color palette can tie them together. You don’t have to go this route, but if
  you’re worried about things looking to wackadoodle, go with this method. You can
  choose artworks that share at least one color, or use frames and mats to create color
  consistency.

  • Vary the Scale and Orientation: Mix different sizes and orientations (landscape,
  portrait, square) to add visual interest. Larger pieces can serve as anchors, while
  smaller works fill in and create a dynamic flow.

  • Balance Styles and Mediums: If you have a bold abstract next to a traditional
  landscape, balance it on another wall with a similarly modern piece. This way, no
  single style or medium overwhelms the space.

  • Create Thematic Links: Look for subtle thematic connections between pieces –
  this could be subject matter, artistic technique, or underlying concept.

  • Consider the Placement: Grouping art thematically or by style can create distinct
  zones in a room, each with its own eclectic charm. Alternatively, a gallery wall mixing
  various pieces can become a focal point.

  • Play with Frames: Using different frames adds to the eclectic vibe, but ensures that
  they don’t clash. You can choose frames of a similar thickness or material to maintain
  some uniformity. You can also unify with your matting choices.
  • Use Lighting Wisely: The way you light your artwork can significantly affect how it
  harmonizes with other pieces in your space. It’s important to consider not just the
  intensity, but also the color temperature of the lighting, and the finish of your light
  fixtures. When selecting a finish for your gallery lights, you have the option to match
  it with the frame and artwork itself or choose a consistent finish that unites all the
  artworks in your space. Alternatively, you can also think about matching your fixture
  finishes with the rest of the fixtures in your home -or opt for something distinctive to
  add an element of contrast. Whatever your choice, make sure that it is in balance and
  harmony with your entire space.

  • Trust Your Instincts: Often, what works best in an eclectic collection is personal
  preference. If it feels right to you, it will likely resonate in your space.

   HOW TO HANG ART PROPERLY




                                                                        theposterclub.com

 When you’re ready to hang your art and are deciding where it’s going to go, think about
 its placement on the wall. If art is hung too high or too low, it can disrupt the harmony of
 the room.

 Typically, you’ll want to place art so that the center of the artwork is positioned at eye
 level, about 57 to 60 inches from the floor. This standard measurement is commonly
 used in galleries and museums and creates a uniform viewing experience.

 Bear in mind that “eye level” is not a one-size-fits-all; it can differ based on the room’s
 use. For instance, in a child’s room, you might hang art lower to align with their sightline.
 In common areas, adhering to the average height helps maintain visual consistency.



ABUNDANT HOME
                                          ARTWORK




   For art placed above furniture pieces like sofas or consoles, aim to hang it about
   6-8 inches above the furniture’s top. This distance helps create a pleasing visual
   connection between the art and the furniture beneath it. Also, be mindful of the
   scale relationship between the furniture and the artwork.

   I like to use the 2:3 ratio we discussed previously, in which it’s good practice to
   balance the scale of objects using this principle, whether it’s furniture, light fixtures,

   THE ART OF THE GALLERY WALL
  I’ll admit that I like to freeball it when it comes to my gallery walls : 0) But that approach
  is not for everyone. Out of all of the hanging methods, gallery walls are the most
  perplexing to most people, so I’m going to provide a little more guidance.

  If you need some structure and guidance on how to create the perfect gallery wall,
  check out the list below to give yourself the best chance at success:

  1. Plan Your Layout: Before you start hammering nails and turning your walls into
  Swiss cheese, lay out your artwork on the floor. Play with different arrangements until
  you find something you like. Keep balance, spacing, and color distribution in mind.

  PRO TIP: Start with the largest piece as your anchor, then arrange smaller pieces
  around it for balance.

  2. Use Templates: Cut out paper templates that match the size of your frames. Tape
  these templates to the wall to visualize your layout, making adjustments as needed.
  This helps you get the placement right before any holes are made. If you want to skip
  the paper, just use painter’s tape to map your layout.

  PRO TIP: Mark where the nail or hook will go on each paper template to make hanging
  the actual frames easier.

  3. Choose a Focal Point: Select one piece to be the focal point of your gallery wall.
  This could be the largest piece or the one with the most striking visual appeal.

  PRO TIP: Hang the focal piece at eye level in the center or off-center for more visual
  interest, then arrange the other pieces around it.

  4. Maintain Consistent Spacing: Keep the spaces between your frames consistent,
  typically 2-3 inches, to create a cohesive look.

  PRO TIP: Use a ruler or a piece of cardboard cut to your desired spacing width as a
  guide between frames.


ABUNDANT HOME
                                         ARTWORK


  5. Mix and Match: Don’t be afraid to mix different frame styles, sizes, and art types. A
  mix of photographs, paintings, and prints can add depth and interest.

  PRO TIP: Balance different frame styles by keeping one element consistent, like color
  or texture.

  6. Use the Right Tools: A level, measuring tape, pencil, and hammer are essential.
  A level ensures your pictures hang straight. A measuring tape and pencil help in
  making precise marks and measurements.

  PRO TIP: A laser level can be incredibly helpful for aligning multiple frames on the
  same horizontal or vertical line.

  7. Consider Using Picture Hanging Strips: If you want to avoid holes in your walls,
  picture hanging strips are a great alternative. They’re especially handy for lighter
  frames and can be easily removed without damaging the wall.

  PRO TIP: Make sure the wall surface and frame back are clean before applying the
  strips for better adherence.

  8. Anchor Heavier Pieces: For larger, heavier frames, use wall anchors or picture
  hooks that can support the weight.

  PRO TIP: Use a stud finder to locate a stud in the wall for hanging heavy frames for
  added support and stability.

  9. Step Back and Adjust: After hanging each piece, take a step back to look at the
  wall from a distance. This helps you see the overall balance and make any necessary
  adjustments.

  PRO TIP: Take a photo of your arrangement; viewing it as a picture can help spot any
  imbalance or awkward spacing.

  10. Have Fun with It: Remember, your gallery wall is a reflection of your style. Don’t
  stress about getting it perfect. It’s all about creating a space that you love.

  PRO TIP: Personalize your gallery wall with unexpected items like shelves, mirrors,
  masks, or 3D art for added dimension and interest.

        THERE’S NO SHAME IN YOUR
                ART GAME

  Let’s wrap up our tour of the art world with something important – your art, your rules!

  You know, we have all experienced a bit of “art shaming” throughout our lives. Have you
  heard about the sociological study where they asked a group of kindergarteners, “How
  many of you are artists?” The response was 100%, across the board! Every child in the
  classrooms felt that they were an artist. They repeated the question up to grade 4. By the
  time those same children had reached 4th grade, only 2% of them thought they had any
  artistic ability. I wonder how many of you feel that way…that you don’t have an artistic bone
  in your body, because you can’t draw a straight line!

  Also, there’s this weird thing about the human condition. Some people love to feel superior.
  And to feel superior, someone has to be inferior, right?!? Some folks think that if your art isn’t
  a one-of-a-kind masterpiece or if it’s a popular print from a store like Target, it’s somehow
  not worthy. What snobs!

  If a piece from Target or Home Goods catches your eye and makes you happy, go for it!
  Don’t let anyone make you feel less about your choice. Remember, we’re all about making
  simple changes to lift the energy of your space, and boost your energy to manifest more. I’m
  here to tell ya – that does not have to be trendy or cost a lot of money!

  Whether it’s that photo of an elephant you’ve seen a 1000 times before at World Market or
  an abstract painting from Studio McGee that you found at Target, what really matters is that
  you love it, it fits your space, and it aligns with the energy you’re trying to create.

  Now, get out there, have a blast selecting your art, and let your imagination run wild – and
  don’t forget to DREAM BIG!
