                            THE 2:3 GOLDEN RULE

   Using the 2:3 ratio in lighting design helps ensure that the light sources are
   appropriately scaled and distributed throughout the room, enhancing both
   functionality and aesthetics. It’s a helpful guideline to achieve a balanced and
   inviting atmosphere in your interior spaces.

   When using decorative lighting fixtures like pendant lights or wall sconces,
   consider their placement and quantity in accordance with the 2:3 ratio. You
   might hang a group of pendant lights above a dining table, ensuring that they
   cover about two-thirds of the table’s length. This creates a visually appealing
   focal point.

   Chandeliers hung at a balanced height (2:3 ratio) can connect a high ceiling to
   the seating area. Keep in mind that the ratio between the coffee table and the
   chandelier is governed by the 2:3 golden rule as well.

                           A DEEPER DIVE

   When we’re thinking of lighting a room, the first thing we want to do is assess the
   space. Start by evaluating the current lighting situation in your home. Identify
   areas that either lack sufficient lighting, or contain lighting that is outdated or
   ineffective.

   Look at your layout and review your furniture plan, as well as the function of the
   room.

   Take note of the natural light sources, such as windows and skylights, and how
   they affect the space throughout the day. Consider the different activities that
   will take place in the room and identify areas that require specific lighting - such
   as task areas and focal points - or that need ambient lighting.

   1. LAYERED LIGHTING:
   To achieve a well-balanced lighting design, incorporate three main layers of
   lighting: ambient, task, and accent. This approach adds depth, functionality, and
   visual interest to your space.

   Use the 2:3 ratio in terms of fixture placement and illumination ratio. For example:
   Pendants should cover two-thirds of the kitchen island, and two-thirds of a work
   surface should be illuminated, etc.


            • Ambient Lighting - Start with ambient lighting, which provides general
            illumination to the entire room. This can be achieved through overhead
            fixtures such as chandeliers, flush-mount or semi-flush mount fixtures,
            or recessed lighting. Wall sconces and track lighting can also be used
            to provide ambient lighting.

            • Task Lighting - Task lighting serves a specific purpose by providing
            focused light for activities like reading, cooking, or working. Examples of
            task lighting include desk lamps, reading lights, under-cabinet lights in
            kitchens, or vanity lights in bathrooms. Place these fixtures strategically
            to ensure adequate lighting for the intended tasks.

            • Accent Lighting - Accent lighting is used to highlight specific features
            or create a focal point within a room. It adds drama and visual interest.
            Consider using accent lighting to showcase artwork, accentuate
            textured walls, or create a cozy ambiance. Options for accent lighting
            include track lighting, picture lights, recessed spotlights, or wall-
            mounted fixtures with adjustable heads.

  2. LED AND BULBS:
  Incandescent bulbs are pretty much a thing of the past and now (for the most part)
  we’re dealing with LED bulbs (or lamps, if you want to be technical about it). LED
  bulbs are energy-efficient, long-lasting, and offer a range of color temperatures.

  Warm white (2700-3000K) bulbs create a cozy and inviting atmosphere, while
  cool white (3500-4100K) bulbs work well for task-oriented spaces. Consider
  the desired mood and functionality of each area when choosing bulb color
  temperatures.

  3. DIMMERS AND CONTROLS:
  Incorporating dimmer switches allows you to adjust the brightness and mood of
  the room. Dimmers are particularly useful in areas like living rooms, bedrooms, and
  dining areas, as they offer flexibility and create ambiance. Additionally, consider
  installing smart lighting systems that enable remote control or programmable
  lighting options, especially in the bedroom.

  4. HIGHLIGHTING ARCHITECTURAL FEATURES:
  Use lighting to draw attention to architectural features like textured walls, exposed
  beams, or artwork. Install wall-mounted spotlights or gallery lights to create focal
  points and add visual interest to your space.

  Create visual harmony by ensuring that the lighting choices in different rooms
  flow harmoniously throughout your home. Consistency in color temperature,
  finish, and style will create a cohesive look and a sense of continuity.

            2. Cabinet Hinges: Hinges are essential for cabinet doors. They can be

            3. Door Hardware: Door hardware encompasses doorknobs, handles,
            hinges, and locks. It’s important to choose door hardware that complements
            the overall design of the space and ensures smooth functionality. Different
            types of doors, like interior, exterior, sliding, or pocket doors, may require
            specific hardware.

            4. Shower Curtain Rings: Choose decorative shower curtain rings or hooks
            to add a touch of elegance to your bathroom without a complete overhaul.

   So consider the following factors to decide what will suit your needs and the
   functionality of your space:

           1. Number of Holes: Check the number of pre-drilled holes in your sink or
           countertop. This will determine the type of faucet you can install. Common
           configurations include single-hole, center-set (4-inch spread), and widespread
           (8-inch spread) faucets. Make sure the faucet you choose matches the existing
           hole configuration!

           2. Faucet Type: In the kitchen and bathroom, you have several faucet types
           to choose from, including single-handle, double-handle, wall-mounted, and
           touchless faucets. Each type has its own set of advantages and considerations.
           For example, a single-handle faucet may offer ease of use and a streamlined
           look, while a double-handle faucet allows for precise control over temperature.

           3. Faucet Size: Consider the size of your sink and countertop. In a small bathroom,
           an oversized faucet might overwhelm the space, while a large kitchen sink may
           require a taller and more substantial faucet to reach comfortably.

           4. Spout Height and Reach: In the bathroom, the height and reach of the faucet’s
           spout can impact its functionality. Taller spouts provide more clearance for
           washing hands and filling containers, while a longer reach can reduce splashing
           and make the sink more accessible.

           5. Sink Depth: The depth of your sink matters when selecting a faucet. A deep
           sink may require a faucet with a higher arc or pull-down sprayer to accommodate
           various tasks and prevent splashing.

           6. Mounting Type: Pay attention to the mounting type of your sink and countertop.
           Some sinks are top-mounted (drop-in), while others are under-mounted or
           integrated (solid-surface). The faucet you choose should be compatible with
           your sink’s mounting style.

           7. Sprayer Function: In the kitchen, if you opt for a faucet with a pull-down or
           pull-out sprayer, consider the size and depth of your sink. A sprayer with a long
           hose can provide flexibility for various cleaning tasks.

          8. Handle Configuration: Check the clearance and space available around
          the faucet handles. Wall-mounted faucets, for instance, may require more
          wall space and need to be positioned correctly.

          9. Installation Height: Confirm the installation height of the faucet,
          especially in bathrooms with vanities. The faucet should comfortably clear
          the sink’s rim or vessel bowl.

          10. Plumbing Connections: Ensure that the faucet’s plumbing connections
          align with your existing plumbing setup. If significant modifications are
          needed, it could add to the installation cost.

          11. Valve Type: The type of valve used in your faucet affects its durability
          and reliability. Ceramic disc valves are known for their longevity and smooth
          operation, reducing the chances of leaks.

          12. Water Flow Rate: Check the flow rate (measured in gallons per minute,
          or GPM) of the faucet. Low-flow faucets are more water-efficient, helping
          you save on water bills and reducing your environmental footprint. Check
          your local requirements as some states have more restrictions than others.

          13. Additional Features: Some faucets come with extra features like built-in
          water filtration, which can improve water quality, or touchless technology,
          which reduces the spread of germs by allowing you to turn the faucet on
          and off without touching it.

          14. Ease of Maintenance: Think about how easy it is to clean and maintain
          the fixtures, and be honest about whether or not you are willing to maintain
          a finish that requires a bit more work. Finishes like polished nickel or chrome
          show off fingerprints and water spots, while finishes like oil-rubbed bronze
          can be hard to keep looking good with hard water and wear over time. If
          you hate cleaning, consider faucets with finishes that resist water spots
          and fingerprints to save you time on upkeep.

  JUST MY TYPE - HOW TO PICK THE RIGHT FAUCET

            • Center-Set Faucets: These faucets have a single spout and two handles
            for hot and cold water. They are a classic choice and can fit well with top-
            mounted sinks. However, they can sometimes look a bit dated.

            • Single-Hole Faucets: If you prefer a sleek and minimalist look, single-hole
            faucets are an excellent option. They have a single handle that controls
            both water temperature and flow.

   SHE’S HIGH MAINTENANCE
   All finishes can be beautiful, but some are a little more high-maintenance than
   others.

   While oil-rubbed bronze (ORB) finishes for fixtures can add a rich and luxurious
   look to your space, there are some downsides to consider:

           • Maintenance: ORB finishes require regular maintenance to keep their
           appearance. They can show fingerprints, water spots, and other marks
           easily, so you may find yourself wiping them down frequently.

           • Scratches: ORB finishes are more prone to scratching and scuffing than
           some other finishes. Sharp or abrasive objects can leave marks on the
           surface, affecting its appearance.

           • Variability: The appearance of oil-rubbed bronze can vary between
           different manufacturers and even between individual pieces. This can
           make it challenging to achieve a consistent look if you’re using multiple
           fixtures from different sources.

           • Limited Compatibility: ORB finishes may not match as well with other
           metals or finishes in your space. If you have a mix of fixtures or hardware with
           different finishes, achieving a cohesive design can be more challenging.

           • Aging: Over time, the ORB finish may change or wear off, particularly in
           areas that receive a lot of use or come into contact with cleaning agents.
           This can result in an uneven or worn appearance.

   Chrome and polished nickel finishes for fixtures have their advantages, but there
   are also some downsides to consider too:

         • Fingerprints and Water Spots: Chrome and polished nickel finishes can show
         fingerprints, water spots, and other smudges quite easily. This means you may need
         to wipe down your fixtures frequently to maintain their shiny appearance.

         • Scratches: While these finishes are generally durable, they can still scratch over
         time, particularly if abrasive cleaning materials or sharp objects are used. Scratches
         can mar the smooth, reflective surface.

         • Matching Challenges: Chrome and polished nickel may not always match perfectly
         with other metals or finishes in your space. If you have a mix of fixtures or hardware
         with different finishes, achieving a cohesive design can be more challenging.

         • Maintenance: While these finishes are relatively low-maintenance compared to
         some others, they still require regular cleaning to keep them looking their best.
         Failure to do so can result in a dull or tarnished appearance.

         • Water Stains: If your water supply contains a high mineral content, you may notice
         mineral deposits or water stains on chrome or polished nickel fixtures. These can be
         more challenging to remove and may require specific cleaning products.



   While gold or brass finishes can add a touch of luxury and sophistication to your
   space, there are some downsides to consider:

        • Maintenance: These finishes require regular cleaning and maintenance to prevent
        tarnishing and water spots. Harsh cleaning products can damage the finish, so you’ll
        need to be gentle when cleaning..

        want.

        cabinet pulls, and lighting fixtures) in the same gold or brass finish can be challenging.

   So, if this is a look that you’re into, here’s how to do it effectively:

          • Establish a Dominant Finish: Start by selecting one dominant hardware
          finish that will be the primary theme throughout your home. This finish will
          serve as the anchor, providing consistency and cohesiveness. Common
          dominant finishes include brushed nickel, brushed brass, chrome, matte
          black, or oil-rubbed bronze.

          • Understand the Color Palette: Consider the overall color palette of your
          space, including walls, flooring, and other design elements. Ensure that your
          chosen finishes complement this palette. For instance, warm finishes like
          brass or gold can work well with warmer color schemes, while cooler finishes
          like chrome or silver suit cooler tones. This is not a hard and fast rule, but it will
          give you some guidance when creating your space.

          • Use Complementary Finishes: To add depth and interest, choose one
          or two complementary finishes that contrast with the dominant one. These
          complementary finishes should be used sparingly and strategically. For
          example, if your dominant finish is chrome, you could introduce matte black
          or brass as complementary finishes.

          • Consider Room Function: Think about the function of each room and how
          hardware finishes can enhance it. For instance, in a modern kitchen, you might
          opt for stainless steel appliances and hardware to match the sleek design. In
          a cozy bedroom, warm and inviting finishes like brass can create a comforting
          atmosphere.

          • Coordinate with Other Elements: Your finishes should coordinate with
          other design elements in the room, such as lighting fixtures, cabinetry, and
          furnishings. Ensure that these elements share similar or complementary
          finishes to maintain harmony in the space.

          • Balance and Distribution: Be mindful of the balance and distribution of
          finishes. Avoid overloading a room with too many finishes, as this can create
          visual chaos. Instead, strategically place hardware with complementary
          finishes to draw attention to specific areas. If you are using black knobs on
          your kitchen cabinets and want to create a focal point or some drama at the
          sink, you could bring in a different finish (like brass) for your sink faucet.
